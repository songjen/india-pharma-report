**【2026-08-25】CDSCO expert committee recommends waiving local Phase III and bioequivalence trials for generic lenacapavir by Dr Reddy's and Emcure** — India's drug regulator CDSCO, through its Subject Expert Committee (SEC) meeting held this month, recommended waiving local Phase III clinical trial and bioequivalence-study requirements for generic lenacapavir (463.5 mg/1.5 ml injection; Gilead-licensed for 120 low- and middle-income countries) proposed by Dr Reddy's Laboratories and Emcure Pharmaceuticals, citing the drug's approval in the US, UK, Canada, EU and Australia. The waiver is conditional: the companies must submit a Phase IV post-marketing efficacy study protocol to CDSCO within three months of receiving marketing authorisation. A waiver for heavily treatment-experienced patients with multidrug-resistant HIV was not recommended due to insufficient evidence on the drug's behaviour in the Indian population. Lenacapavir is a breakthrough twice-yearly injection for HIV pre-exposure prophylaxis (PrEP); the decision will significantly accelerate its access in India. — Source: [ET Pharma](https://economictimes.com/industry/healthcare/biotech/pharmaceuticals/lenacapavir-drl-emcure-get-approval-to-make-breakthrough-hiv-drug/articleshow/133476844.cms)

**【2026-08-24】Health Ministry announces medical device regulatory reforms: simplified outsourced sterilisation, uniform testing fees, EU added to recognised jurisdictions for clinical investigation waiver** — The Ministry of Health and Family Welfare announced amendments to the Medical Devices Rules, 2017 in three areas: ① simplification of provisions on outsourced sterilisation — manufacturers lacking in-house sterilisation facilities will no longer need a separate loan licence for sterilisation activities, easing compliance, documentation and costs; ② insertion of a Ninth Schedule prescribing uniform fees for testing by Medical Device Testing Laboratories, establishing a standardised and transparent fee structure; ③ amendment of Rule 63 to include the European Union in the list of recognised stringent regulatory jurisdictions for waiver of clinical investigation requirements for devices without predicate devices (previously only the US, UK, Australia, Canada and Japan), enabling faster access to eligible EU-approved devices in India. — Source: [Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/industry/healthcare/biotech/healthcare/health-ministry-announces-medical-device-regulatory-reforms-for-ease-of-doing-business/articleshow/133458124.cms)

**【2026-08-24】Indian Pharmacopoeia Commission (IPC) drafts dedicated semaglutide monograph for IP Addendum 2028** — India is establishing official quality benchmarks for semaglutide (the key ingredient in blockbuster GLP-1 drugs Ozempic/Wegovy): the IPC, the health ministry body setting national drug standards, is drafting a dedicated pharmacopoeial monograph to be included in the upcoming Indian Pharmacopoeia (IP) Addendum 2028, defining legally binding standards for identity, purity, potency and testing. Indian firms produce the drug at scale via two routes — chemical peptide synthesis and recombinant DNA cell culture — and the national monograph will give state regulators and testing labs a binding statutory benchmark to enforce uniform limits on purity, potency and process-specific impurities, while helping detect counterfeits and cutting testing costs. Since the compound patent expired on 20 March 2026, at least six Indian drugmakers (Sun Pharma, Zydus, Dr Reddy's, Mankind, Torrent, Glenmark) have launched cheaper generics, with monthly prices around ₹1,300–16,400; CareEdge expects India's weight-loss drug market to cross $347 million by 2035. — Source: [Mint/Livemint](https://www.livemint.com/news/india-setting-official-standards-and-blueprint-for-weight-loss-drug-semaglutide-11787459347884.html)
