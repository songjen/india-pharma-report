## 1. Government Pharma Policy

**【2026-09-22】DGTR initiates anti-dumping investigation into Glycine from China PR** — India's Directorate General of Trade Remedies launched an anti-dumping probe into imports of Glycine — a food flavour enhancer and pharmaceutical ingredient — from China PR, following an application by domestic manufacturer Avid Organics. The injury assessment will consider data from 2022–25; the applicant alleges material injury from dumped Chinese imports and has sought anti-dumping duty. **For SINOBRIGHT: China-related raw-material investigations have extended from chemical intermediates to amino-acid pharmaceutical basics, tightening origin and transhipment evidence requirements for comparable products.** — Source: [The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/business/india-begins-anti-dumping-probes-against-imports-of-glycine-chemical-from-china/article71497044.ece)【Source: DGTR Initiation Notification 2026-09-22】

**【2026-09-22】CDSCO publishes August surveillance results: 220 NSQ batches, one spurious sample** — The Central Drugs Laboratory flagged 29 samples and State Drugs Testing Laboratories 191 samples as not of standard quality, while one spurious sample misusing another company's brand was detected in Delhi and is under investigation. Failures centred on sterility and bacterial endotoxin in injectables and on dissolution in tablets, covering products such as amoxicillin-clavulanate tablets and amikacin; the DCGI put the recent non-conformity rate at about 1.5%. **For SINOBRIGHT: customer scrutiny of upstream CoA, impurity profiles and batch-to-batch consistency documentation will tighten further.** — Source: [Hindustan Times](https://www.hindustantimes.com/india-news/cdsco-finds-one-spurious-drug-sample-in-delhi-220-nsqs-in-aug-surveillance-101790074138180.html)【Source: CDSCO NSQ & Spurious Drug Alert, August 2026】

**【2026-09-21】CDSCO circular tightens prescribing and retail control of painkillers and antibiotics** — The circular requires NSAIDs and antibiotics to be used only when clinically indicated, at the appropriate dose and duration and under a registered practitioner's prescription, and reiterates that Schedule H/H1 drugs must not be sold at retail without prescription. Pharmacists, retailers and distributors must comply with statutory sales and record-keeping requirements and must not facilitate self-medication; state drug authorities are directed to raise awareness and enforce compliance. **For SINOBRIGHT: tighter antibiotic channel enforcement in India may dampen downstream restocking.** — Source: [LiveMint](https://www.livemint.com/news/cdsco-flags-indiscriminate-use-of-painkillers-and-antibiotics-11790088574575.html)【Source: CDSCO circular 2026-09-21】

**【2026-09-22】Supreme Court hears drug pricing PIL, lists matter for 29 September** — The Court noted that an essential cancer medicine carries an MRP of ₹27,000 against a price to retailer of only ₹2,700, calling it "broad daylight dacoity" against patients. The petitioner submitted that roughly 82% of medicines are non-scheduled under the Drugs (Prices Control) Order, 2013, with no upfront launch-price control; the Indian Pharmaceutical Alliance told the Court that margins accrue mainly at the distribution end. The Bench will examine initial price fixation and mandatory generic prescribing on 29 September. — Source: [The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/broad-daylight-dacoity-says-supreme-court-on-overpricing-of-essential-cancer-drugs/article71497051.ece)
