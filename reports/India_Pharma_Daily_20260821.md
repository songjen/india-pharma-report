## 1. Government Pharma Policy

**【2026-08-19】Parliamentary Panel Recommends 20% Cap on Gap Between Landing Price and MRP of Drugs & Medical Devices**
The Parliamentary Standing Committee on Health and Family Welfare, in its 176th Report titled *Affordability and Accessibility of Healthcare Facilities in Public and Private Sector*, has recommended that the gap between the landing price of a medicine or medical device and its Maximum Retail Price (MRP) should not exceed 20%, aiming to compress supply-chain mark-ups and reduce out-of-pocket spending. The report cited the clot-busting drug Tenecteplase as an example: its landing cost is about ₹18,000 (≈$188) while its MRP is as high as ₹50,000 (≈$522), with patients billed at MRP. The panel also called for rationalising prices of peripheral stents and drug-eluting balloons, noting that the 2017 coronary stent price cap (from ~₹1.9 lakh down to ₹25,000–30,000, ≈$261–313) generated estimated annual consumer savings of ₹13,353 crore (≈$1.39 billion). The recommendation is at the parliamentary committee level and awaits government decision; if adopted, it would squeeze margins across the domestic drug/device distribution chain. — Source: [The Times of India](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/india/panel-seeks-20-cap-on-drug-medical-device-mark-ups/articleshow/133337330.cms)
