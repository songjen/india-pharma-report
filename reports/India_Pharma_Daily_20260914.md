## 1. Government Pharma Policy

**【2026-09-11】India-EU FTA tabled before the EU Council for signature authorisation; pharma tariffs to be largely abolished** — On 11 September the European Commission formally put forward its proposals to the Council of the European Union for the signature and conclusion of the India-EU Free Trade Agreement, seeking authorisation to sign. Negotiations concluded on 27 January 2026; if the Council clears the proposal, the pact will be signed and then require the European Parliament's consent before conclusion and entry into force. Both sides target signature before end-2026 and implementation in H1 2027, under an "accelerated procedure" (texts submitted in English first, other official EU languages to follow — France has objected on multilingualism grounds). Under the deal, tariffs on 96% of EU goods exports to India will be eliminated or reduced (saving EU exporters around €4 billion a year), with **customs duties on machinery, chemical products and pharmaceutical products largely abolished**; India secures preferential access to 97% of EU tariff lines covering 99.5% of trade value, with more than 70% of tariff lines getting immediate zero duty. Annual India-EU goods and services trade already exceeds €180 billion; the pact is the largest trade agreement ever concluded by either side. — Source: [The Hindu BusinessLine](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/economy/eu-tables-india-fta-for-council-signature-move-closer-to-landmark-deal/article71456134.ece)

**【2026-09-13】Science Minister Jitendra Singh: India's bioeconomy reached USD 195.3 billion in 2025; vaccine value-chain capabilities upgraded across the board** — Union Minister of State (Independent Charge) for Science and Technology Jitendra Singh, addressing a high-level reception in New Delhi for the Coalition for Epidemic Preparedness Innovations (CEPI) Board and Investors Council, said India has moved beyond being a vaccine manufacturer to a high-end biomedical system spanning vaccine technologies, complex biologicals and epidemic preparedness: the bioeconomy reached USD 195.3 billion in 2025, up 18% year-on-year; the biotech startup ecosystem expanded to 11,855 startups (1,780 added during the year); biopharma accounts for nearly 30% of the bioeconomy. India supplies vaccines to more than 100 countries, accounts for nearly 60% of global vaccine production and meets more than 65% of WHO routine immunisation requirements. Under the India-centric CEPI Mission (Ind-CEPI), India has developed GEMCOVAC-19, its first mRNA COVID-19 vaccine (Emergency Use Authorisation granted); an inactivated Chikungunya vaccine candidate is in Phase II/III; monkeypox and pan-sarbecovirus multivalent self-assembled nanocage candidates are at advanced pre-clinical stage; the BRIC-THSTI Bioassay Laboratory is one of CEPI's 17 centralised network laboratories and has supported eight vaccine candidates and tested over 100,000 samples. — Source: [Prokerala/IANS](https://www.prokerala.com/news/articles/a1811773.html)

## 2. Pharma Industry News

**【2026-09-13】Major counterfeit drug network busted in Bengaluru: cancer drugs and ICU injections reached over 90 hospitals** — Bengaluru Police's Special Investigation Team (SIT) and the Drugs Control Department raided Krupa Healthcare near Minerva Circle on 12 September, seizing a large stock of suspected counterfeit medicines and arresting owner Veeresh Kumar Jain. Investigators say the network operated on two models: low-cost medicines were rebranded and sold under the labels of reputed pharma companies, and expired medicines were repackaged into new containers with forged labels; the products were supplied to more than 90 hospitals and clinics in Bengaluru at discounts of up to about 50%, including cancer drugs, life-saving medicines and ICU injections, with more than 15 medical representatives involved in distribution. The case stems from an 18 August raid on farmhouses in Bidadi/Dodderi that recovered suspected counterfeit medicines worth about ₹4.91 crore along with injection-filling equipment and counterfeit labels; coordinated searches followed at more than 10 locations across Karnataka, Haryana, Himachal Pradesh, Tamil Nadu and Maharashtra, tracing back to Krupa Healthcare. Suspected batch numbers have been blocked on the Karnataka FDA portal and samples sent for laboratory analysis; alleged kingpin Prashant remains absconding and Jain is the only person arrested so far. — Source: [The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/karnataka/large-stock-of-counterfeit-medicines-seized-from-private-facility-in-bengaluru/article71459863.ece)

**【2026-09-12】Maharashtra FDA complaints jump 557% to 1,397 in three months; 677 licences suspended** — The Maharashtra Food and Drug Administration received 1,397 drug-related complaints between June and August 2026, a 557% rise over the 2025-26 quarterly average of about 213. It investigated 924 complaints (up 378% over the quarterly average), conducted 2,902 inspections, seized material worth ₹11.41 crore (up 234%), suspended 677 licences, cancelled 203 and registered 20 FIRs, with action taken in 880 cases. The largest share of complaints concerned allopathic medicines sold without prescriptions, followed by Ayurvedic medicines sold with unauthorised claims. Inspection findings included absence of competent technical staff, unauthorised personnel making entries in batch records, testing by unqualified QC staff, failure to prepare Annual Product Quality Reviews (APQR), discrepancies in sterilisation and autoclave records, missing employee medical and vendor audit records, mismatches between batch records and actual production or material weights, and manufacture of Not of Standard Quality (NSQ) drugs or products outside approved licences. In manufacturing, 123 allopathic units were inspected with action against 58 (13 suspensions, 45 cancellations); of 301 Ayurvedic units inspected, 31 faced action. — Source: [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/maharashtra-fda-complaints-surge-557-per-cent-to-1397-in-3-months-as-drug-enforcement-intensifies)

## 4. Risk Alerts

- **Counterfeit and illegal-channel risk is surfacing sharply as Indian enforcement enters a high-frequency phase**: a Bengaluru network supplied rebranded/expired medicines with forged labels to more than 90 hospitals and clinics (about ₹4.91 crore seized in a single case), while Maharashtra FDA logged a 557% jump in complaints in three months and suspended 677 licences. For bona fide suppliers of APIs and formulations into India, grey-market goods and diverted stock damage brand and price structures and also raise end-customer demands for traceable sourcing and verifiable batch numbers — engagement on authorised-channel control, batch traceability and anti-counterfeiting (QR codes/security marks) should be stepped up in parallel.
- **The India-EU FTA is entering the countdown to signature, with pharma tariffs to be largely abolished**: if signed as targeted before end-2026 and implemented in H1 2027, EU pharma export duties into India and Indian export barriers into the EU will both fall sharply — EU pharmaceutical products will enter India more easily while Indian formulations gain competitiveness in Europe, so the relative cost and access position of Chinese and Indian APIs/intermediates in both the EU and Indian markets needs reassessment.
- **The rupee remains above 95.5, keeping import-cost and FX pressure on Indian customers**: USD/INR was about 95.55 in the morning of 14 September. For an Indian pharma industry heavily dependent on imported APIs, intermediates and KSMs, a weak rupee directly raises the local-currency cost of imported raw materials, and buyer pressure on pricing and extended payment terms in near-term negotiations is likely to stay firm.
