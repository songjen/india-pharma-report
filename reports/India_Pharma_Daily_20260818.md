## 1. Government Pharma Policy

### 【2026-08-17】Telangana launches TG SAFE: Food Safety and Drugs Control merged into unified enforcement authority (effective Aug 15) (India Today/Indian Pharma Post)
The Telangana government formally established the Telangana Standards Authority for Food and Essential Drugs (TG SAFE) under G.O. Ms. No. 67 of the Health, Medical & Family Welfare Department (signed Aug 4, effective Aug 15), merging the Food Safety Department and the Drugs Control Administration (DCA) under one administrative umbrella: six wings — Drug Administration, Food Safety, Enforcement, Legal & Prosecution, Laboratory, and Administration. Police personnel will be attached to TG SAFE to support enforcement, with **intelligence-led, risk-based inspections**. Operations begin as a pilot in the CURE region of Hyderabad (high density of food businesses and drug establishments), and a state-level committee will assess modernisation outcomes before wider rollout. DCA Director General Avinash Mohanty doubles as TG SAFE Commissioner. Following the Maharashtra FDA crackdown on Ayurvedic manufacturers (Aug 12), this marks another escalation in state-level drug regulation — licensing, inspection and documentation requirements for API and formulation manufacturers are tightening across India.

### 【2026-08-17】Sun Pharma wins comprehensive US Lipitor antitrust victory: Third Circuit affirms summary judgment and denies class certification (The Hindu/Business Standard)
The US Court of Appeals for the Third Circuit affirmed the District Court's **summary judgment entirely in favour of Sun Pharma and its subsidiaries** and upheld the denial of class certification for plaintiffs, substantially closing the antitrust litigation over the 2008 Pfizer-Ranbaxy patent settlement concerning generic Lipitor (atorvastatin). Plaintiffs had alleged the settlement unlawfully delayed generic entry. The ruling removes a long-standing legal overhang dating to Sun's 2014 acquisition of Ranbaxy and clears a major litigation risk for its US generics business. It also carries a signal for Indian manufacturers pursuing Paragraph IV strategies: antitrust scrutiny of Hatch-Waxman settlement terms remains a live risk category requiring early legal planning.
