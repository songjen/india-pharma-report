## 1. Government Pharma Policy

**【2026-09-02】India's Narcotics Control Bureau plans a voluntary code of conduct for precursor chemicals to curb diversion into illicit drug-making — running parallel to the biologics-vs-synthetics classification push** — Mint reports that India's Narcotics Control Bureau (NCB, under the Home Ministry) is drafting a voluntary code of conduct for participants in the precursor-chemical supply chain, using end-to-end tracking and auditing to prevent diversion of legal chemicals into illicit drug manufacture. Running in parallel is the health ministry's plan to force companies to distinguish "biologics vs synthetic drugs" and declare raw-material origin on licence applications and packaging (the latter was covered in yesterday's report). If adopted, the precursor code would import "supply-chain due diligence + record auditing" obligations into precursor/starting-material trade — for Chinese suppliers exporting KSM, key intermediates and solvents to India, customer due-diligence and documentation requirements could tighten. Track the code's text and industry feedback. — Source: [Mint coverage (via The Balanced News)](https://thebalanced.news/politics/bc89cfbd-7a52-4087-937d-5ea88224a8ea/india-proposes-regulatory-measures-for-precursor-chemicals-and-drug-classification)

**【2026-09-02】India proposes to amend the Drugs Rules, 1945: small-volume drug imports to move from licence to online intimation; imported-drug shelf-life requirement to be raised to ≥12 months on arrival** — The Union Ministry of Health and Family Welfare has opened a public consultation on amendments to the Drugs Rules, 1945 to streamline drug import clearance and revise shelf-life compliance: (i) import licences for small quantities of drugs for testing, analysis and non-clinical trials would be abolished and replaced by an online prior-intimation system, with the system receipt enabling import — except for high-risk categories such as sex hormones, cytotoxic drugs, β-lactams, live-microorganism biologics and narcotics/psychotropics; (ii) the current "remaining shelf life ≥60% of total" standard would be unified to "remaining shelf life ≥12 months from entry into India", with biologics and radiopharmaceuticals exempted and all other quality/safety/efficacy provisions unchanged. This extends India's January 2026 domestic R&D deregulation (abolition of small-batch trial-production licences and prior approval for low-risk BE studies, replaced by online intimation) to the import side. For companies exporting pharmaceuticals/formulations to India or undertaking CRO/BE work there, batch planning, inventory turnover and customs filing will be directly affected — track the fine print and effective date from official Indian notifications, and strictly comply with high-risk-category requirements. — Source: [Shandong Provincial Commerce Dept early-warning](http://commerce.shandong.gov.cn/art/2026/9/2/art_250176_10378354.html)

**【2026-09-03】Pharmexcil targets $1.4 billion in export business at iPHEX 2026 (Sep 7–9, New Delhi); India's Q1 FY27 pharma exports up 6.8%, June up 7.3%** — The Pharmaceuticals Export Promotion Council of India (Pharmexcil, under the Commerce Ministry) said the 12th edition of iPHEX (Sep 7–9, Bharat Mandapam, New Delhi) will focus on converting buyer-seller meetings into export orders, targeting around **$1.4 billion** in export opportunities, with 800+ exhibitors, 600+ overseas companies and ~30,000 business visitors expected across formulations, APIs, biologicals, vaccines, biosimilars and medical devices. The council disclosed that despite war-related supply-chain shocks (Strait of Hormuz shipping closures, air-cargo disruptions) becoming the industry's "new normal," India's pharma exports grew **6.8%** YoY in Q1 FY27 (Apr–Jun) — versus full-year growth of just 2.13% last fiscal — with June alone up 7.3%. Pharmexcil Chairman Namit Joshi also said dependence on Chinese APIs will not fall in the near term but the relationship is moving "from competition to collaboration," and called for "guardrails" so PLI beneficiaries keep investing in India. — Source: [Rediff Money (PTI)](https://money.rediff.com/news/market/pharmexcil-targets-1-4b-export-biz-via-iphex-2026/53692820260903), [Indian Pharma Post](https://www.indianpharmapost.com/news/iphex-2026-pharmexcil-eyes-14-billion-pharma-export-boost-21689)

## 2. Pharma Industry News

**【2026-09-03】Monsoon illnesses and a sixfold surge in H1N1 lift India's anti-infective demand: July anti-infective/antibacterial sales +12% MoM** — PharmaTrac data show Indian July anti-infective sales rose to ₹2,359 crore ≈ **$250 million** (from ₹2,107 crore in June) and antibacterials to ₹2,109 crore ≈ **$223 million** (from ₹1,883 crore) — both up ~12% MoM and ~6% YoY; gastrointestinal sales rose ~1.8% MoM to ₹2,081 crore ≈ $220 million. With H1N1 cases up more than sixfold versus prior years on top of seasonal dengue, typhoid, malaria and chikungunya peaks, flu-vaccine demand has spiked — Tata 1mg says August vaccine sales were nearly three times last year's and more than double July's, with supply constraints in several cities. **【2026-09-01】USFDA warning letter to Reliance Life Sciences (Navi Mumbai): falsified microbiology lab records + missing LIMS dilution factors; products placed on Import Alert 66-40** — Based on a February 19–27, 2026 inspection (FEI 3009510975), the USFDA issued a warning letter (No. 320-26-117, dated August 18; made public September 1) to Reliance Life Sciences' Navi Mumbai facility: environmental-monitoring and additional sample plates logged as "collected and incubated" could not be located in incubators, analyst interviews confirmed samples were never collected, and CFU counts were entered as if incubation were ongoing — a pattern FDA characterised as systematic underreporting of microbial-contamination risk. In addition, the facility's LIMS omitted required dilution factors from microbial-limit test calculations, releasing batches against raw plate-count averages rather than concentration-adjusted compendial values. FDA deemed the products adulterated under FD&C Act 501(a)(2)(B) and placed all drug products from the site under **Import Alert 66-40** effective July 9, effectively blocking US shipments; the company's March 20 response to the Form 483 was found insufficient. Following Eugia and Cipla events, this case again confirms USFDA treats "lab data-integrity + LIMS configuration" deficiencies as systemic failures — Indian API/formulation and CDMO clients shipping to the US should proactively audit their microbiology testing and data-governance chains. — Source: [Pharma Now](https://www.pharmanow.live/latest-news/reliance-life-sciences-fda-warning-letter-microbiology-lab-falsification-2026)

## 4. Risk Alerts

1. **US MFN pricing pacts expand again; Sep 29 Section 232 tariff deadline approaches (escalated)** — On September 1, Trump announced nine additional mid-size drugmakers joining Medicaid most-favored-nation (MFN) pricing agreements — Alcon, Astellas, BeOne Medicines, BridgeBio, CSL, Kyowa Kirin, Sun Pharma, Teva and UCB — bringing the total to 26 companies covering ~90% of the US prescription-drug market (the White House says the deals are projected to save more than $600 billion); the MFN model is spreading from brand pharma to generic/specialty players including Indian majors. Un-signed companies' patented/innovative drugs and covered ingredients face Section 232 tariffs of 100% from **September 29** (17 big firms have been subject since July 31); the 100% (from Aug 2028) and 200% (from Aug 2029) tariff schedule on imported generics is unchanged. Whether Aurobindo, Lupin and Dr. Reddy's follow suit is the next watch point.
2. **USFDA inspection intensity on India stays high, focused on data integrity (continued, escalated)** — The new Reliance Life Sciences warning letter (see Industry News above), on top of recent Eugia and Cipla Pithampur events, confirms US "zero-tolerance" treatment of lab data-integrity and LIMS-type deficiencies as systemic failures; clients with US-facing product lines — and the CDMO/API links supplying them — should run proactive self-audits of microbiology testing and data governance.
3. **India's proposed import shelf-life tightening (pending formal adoption)** — Changing the imported-drug remaining-shelf-life floor from "≥60% of total" to "≥12 months from entry," plus moving small-volume R&D imports to online intimation, would directly affect batch planning, near-expiry inventory and customs filings for exports to India; exporters should align compliance ledgers once official notifications are issued.
4. **NPPA pricing enforcement and the "manufacturer sole liability" proposal (continued)** — The parliamentary committee's cost-plus-value hybrid pricing debate continues (2,737 overpricing cases filed and ~₹1,582 crore ≈ $167 million recovered to date, as previously disclosed); media also report NPPA is weighing making manufacturers solely liable for downstream overpricing, keeping pricing-compliance pressure on India-facing sellers intact.
