## 1. Government Pharma Policy

**[2026-11-20] DGTR extends questionnaire-response deadlines in the Penicillin G and 6-APA cases to 30 November, with no further extension** — The Penicillin G and its salts case (F. No. 6/56/2026-DGTR, initiated 28 Sep 2026, China PR only) and the 6-Aminopenicillanic Acid case (F. No. 6/58/2026-DGTR, initiated 25 Sep 2026, China PR and the EU) had a response deadline of 22 November. Following repeat requests from interested parties, both were extended to 30 November. The letters state that no further extension will be granted and that, under Rule 6(8) of the AD Rules 1995, findings may be recorded on the basis of facts available where a party fails to respond or cooperate. — Source: [DGTR 6-APA letter](https://dgtr.gov.in/sites/default/files/2026-11/Letter%20regarding%20further%20extension%20of%20time%20for%20QR%20filing%206APA.pdf) · [DGTR Penicillin G letter](https://dgtr.gov.in/sites/default/files/2026-11/Letter%20regarding%20further%20extension%20of%20time%20for%20QR%20filing%20PenG.pdf) 【Basis: F. No. 6/58/2026-DGTR and F. No. 6/56/2026-DGTR, 2026-11-20】

**[2026-11-19] Health Ministry issues official note on bringing antimicrobials under the "new drug" definition, G.S.R. 983(E), with a 30-day comment window** — The draft inserts antimicrobials into the definition of new drug under Rule 2(1)(w)(v) of the New Drugs and Clinical Trials Rules 2019, requiring manufacturers to obtain Central Licensing Authority permission before applying to the State Licensing Authority for a manufacturing licence. The Ministry cites quality risk where antimicrobials were approved without bioavailability/bioequivalence and stability studies. Objections may be filed with the Drugs Division or by email within 30 days of the gazette being made public (gazette dated 6 November, window closing in early December). — Source: [PIB](https://pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2338243) · [eGazette](https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2026/278020.pdf) 【Basis: G.S.R. 983(E), 2026-11-06; PIB PRID 2338243】

**[2026-11-23] Karnataka retailers seek a 12-month transition period for GSDP implementation** — The Karnataka Pharma Retailers and Distributors Organisation backs the 4 November gazette draft that inserts Good Storage and Distribution Practices into the Drugs Rules 1945 and makes GDP mandatory for manufacturers, importers and traders, but asks for proportionate compliance relief for traditional pharmacies and a level playing field between retailers and wholesalers. — Source: [pharmabiz](https://www.pharmabiz.com/NewsDetails.aspx?aid=192074&sid=2) 【Basis: Drugs Rules 1945 amendment draft, Gazette of India, 2026-11-04】

## 2. Industry Developments

**[2026-11-23] CPHI India 2026 opens in New Delhi with APIs, intermediates and CDMO as the three main axes** — The show runs 23–25 November at IICC (Yashobhoomi), Dwarka; PMEC India runs 24–26 November at IEML, Greater Noida, creating a dual-venue format. Organisers expect over 2,000 exhibitors, more than 50,000 trade visitors and participants from over 120 countries, covering APIs, finished formulations, excipients, fine chemicals, intermediates and contract services. — Source: [Indian Pharma Post](https://www.indianpharmapost.com/news/cphi-india-2026-to-bring-global-pharma-industry-to-delhi-with-focus-on-apis-formulations-and-cdmo-22444)

**[2026-11-21] IPM MAT sales reach INR 2.78 lakh crore, up 13.19%; anti-infectives grow only 5.7%, the weakest of the top four** — Per the IQVIA October report, MAT sales stood at INR 2,77,735.9 crore (about USD 29.9 bn). October sales were INR 24,988.53 crore, up 11.69% YoY but down 1.1% sequentially. Chronic therapies grew 16.6% on MAT versus 10.9% for acute. By therapy: cardiac INR 37,793.5 crore (+16.8%), gastro-intestinal INR 28,779.2 crore (+11.4%), anti-infectives INR 27,396.5 crore (+5.7%), anti-diabetic INR 25,581.5 crore (+17.7%). — Source: [pharmabiz](https://www.pharmabiz.com/NewsDetails.aspx?aid=192050&sid=2) 【Basis: IQVIA Market Feedback Report, October 2026】

**[2026-11-21] Telangana's TG SAFE issues show-cause notices to 90 retail outlets over prescription-less antibiotic sales** — The 19 November drive was run jointly by the drugs wing, enforcement wing and excise officials across 14 high-risk areas in Hyderabad including Koti, Abids and Basheerbagh, using decoy purchases. Violations included sale of Schedule H1/X products and antibiotics without a valid prescription, dispensing without a registered pharmacist, absence of prescription and H1 registers, and sales without bills. — Source: [pharmabiz](https://www.pharmabiz.com/NewsDetails.aspx?aid=192049&sid=2)

**[2026-11-21] Biotransformation framed as a sustainable route for API and intermediate manufacturing** — Advances in enzyme engineering, strain optimisation and bioprocess development allow APIs and advanced intermediates to be made through more selective, lower-waste routes, which industry experts see as a way to cut chemical waste, improve quality and reset cost structures for selected products. — Source: [pharmabiz](https://www.pharmabiz.com/NewsDetails.aspx?aid=192053&sid=2)

**[2026-11-20] Himachal Pradesh and Uttar Pradesh drug regulators unearth an illegal manufacturing site; 67,000 antibiotic tablets seized** — Acting on substandard samples traced in Uttar Pradesh, inspectors found an unlicensed facility at Rajpura, Nalagarh (Solan district), seizing about 67,000 antibiotic tablets, some 6,500 capsules of pregabalin 300 mg and about 100 kg of printed aluminium foil bearing other companies' branding. Two persons on site were arrested under the Drugs and Cosmetics Act 1940. — Source: [pharmabiz](https://www.pharmabiz.com/NewsDetails.aspx?aid=192057&sid=2)
