## 1. Government Pharma Policy

**【2026-08-31】India-Brazil 8th Trade Monitoring Mechanism: Brazil to widen pharma market access; $30bn bilateral trade target by 2030** — The Ministry of Commerce (MOCI) stated on Monday, August 31, that at the 8th India-Brazil Trade Monitoring Mechanism (TMM) meeting in Brasilia, co-chaired by Commerce Secretary Rajesh Agarwal and Brazil's Secretary of Foreign Trade Tatiana Lacerda Prazeres, **Brazil agreed to widen market access for Indian pharmaceutical products**. India emphasised the need for "more predictable regulatory pathways and greater market access," reiterating its contribution to affordable, quality medicines and accessible healthcare. Both sides reaffirmed the $30 billion bilateral trade target by 2030 (bilateral trade reached $15.07bn in 2025-26), with pharmaceuticals, chemicals, engineering goods and machinery identified as priority sectors. The cooperation builds on the CDSCO-ANVISA MoU signed in February 2026. The two sides also committed to early finalisation of Terms of Reference to expand the India-Mercosur Preferential Trade Agreement (in force since 2009, covering only 450 tariff lines) — India-Mercosur trade reached $20.84bn in 2025. Following Argentina's Aug 29 commitment to upgrade India from Annex II to Annex I, this marks another milestone in India pharma's "Latin America access" storyline, with direct implications for Indian generics/API exports to the region (and competitive dynamics vs Chinese API suppliers). — Source: [The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/india-calls-for-more-predictable-regulatory-environment-in-brazil-to-boost-pharma-exports/article71409833.ece) · [CNBC TV18](https://www.cnbctv18.com/economy/india-brazil-reaffirm-30-billion-trade-target-by-2030-brazil-to-widen-pharma-market-access-19980191.htm)

**【2026-08-29】NPPA proposes making drugmakers solely liable for overpricing of essential medicines; retailers/hospitals could be exempted; industry opposes** — ET Pharma reported on Aug 29 that the National Pharmaceutical Pricing Authority (NPPA) has proposed shifting full responsibility for overpricing of NLEM essential medicines onto manufacturers, deleting the DPCO 2013 Para 26 provision that allowed action against dealers/retailers with proven knowledge of price reductions (extending to nursing homes and hospitals). The revision stems from a draft DPCO 2013 amendment first proposed in 2016, advanced at a senior officials' meeting this week. Industry sources said manufacturers will oppose this "unilateral and unfair" move — even wilful downstream overpricing would escape prosecution. Data context: through March 2026, NPPA levied cumulative penalties of ₹8,447cr (~$888mn), of which ₹1,582cr (~$166mn) was recovered from manufacturers, across 2,737 pricing-violation cases; a parliamentary committee has also urged shifting from the market-based (average retailer margin) formula toward a cost+value hybrid. If implemented, NLEM manufacturers would bear full-chain pricing compliance responsibility, raising pricing and channel-management costs. — Source: [ET Pharma](https://pharma.economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/policy-and-regulations/drugmakers-may-bear-sole-responsibility-for-overpricing-essential-medicines-under-nppa-proposal/133603285)
