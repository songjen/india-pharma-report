## 1. Government Pharma Policy

**【2026-09-10】India plans to bring all sterile drugs under central licensing (CLAA) after ~240 injection batches failed quality tests in FY26** — The Union government plans to shift all sterile drug products — injectables and other sterile dosage forms — from state licensing to the Central License Approving Authority (CLAA) system, requiring amendments to the Drugs Rules, 1945, in order to replace fragmented approvals with uniform requirements for approval and inspection nationwide. According to a government document, about 240 injection samples were found not of standard quality (NSQ) in 2025-26, excluding other sterile dosage forms; the document also cited contamination reported in metoprolol injection and an adenosine injection solution. Currently the central licensing framework covers only selected sterile products — large-volume parenterals, sera and vaccines, and certain cell, stem-cell, gene and recombinant DNA-derived products — while other sterile medicines remain with state authorities. The proposal would bring all sterile drug products under CLAA via the ONDLS portal. India's generic injectable market was valued at about US$3 billion in 2024 and is estimated to reach US$7.6 billion by 2033 (IBEF). The Indian Pharmaceutical Alliance (IPA) Secretary General Sudarshan Jain backed the direction. — Source: [Livemint](https://www.livemint.com/news/govt-plans-central-licensing-for-all-sterile-drugs-after-240-injection-samples-fail-quality-tests-medicine-11789033517269.html)

**【2026-09-10】iPHEX 2026 concludes; Pharmexcil projects US$4.5–5 billion in potential trade deals** — The 12th International Exhibition on Pharma and Healthcare (iPHEX 2026, New Delhi) concluded on 10 September, with organisers Pharmexcil (Pharmaceuticals Export Promotion Council of India) projecting US$4.5–5 billion in commercial negotiations initiated at the event. Over the three days there were more than 8,500 business meetings, 468 overseas buyers from 128 countries and 405 domestic sellers, with total footfall exceeding 50,000. The product mix discussed was about 39% therapeutics, 35.1% formulations and 25.8% active pharmaceutical ingredients (APIs). Official procurement agencies from 15 countries, including the Philippines, Kenya, Cuba and Zambia, attended. Pharmexcil Chairman Namit Joshi said the focus is now on converting discussions into sustained commercial ties. The 13th edition will be held on 19–21 August 2027. — Source: [Rediff/PTI](https://money.rediff.com/news/market/iphex-2026-initiates-5-bn-potential-pharma-trade-deals/54124120260910)

**【2026-09-10】Goyal: India–Russia BIT fast-tracked; pharmaceuticals named a priority area for non-energy trade** — Commerce and Industry Minister Piyush Goyal told the India–Russia Business Dialogue that pharmaceuticals, textiles, engineering goods, food products, chemicals and auto products are key areas for expanding non-energy trade with Russia, to meet a target of US$100 billion in bilateral trade (from a ~US$60 billion base) and US$50 billion in two-way investment by 2030. The India–Russia priority investment projects mechanism is tracking 40 live projects across advanced manufacturing, energy, mining, railways and emerging technologies. Goyal said a new bilateral investment treaty is being fast-tracked to give investors legal certainty, and that local currency settlement mechanisms will be strengthened, noting that "payment friction more often than tariffs is what slows down trade." He urged Russian industry to "manufacture in India, don't just sell in India." — Source: [Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/india/india-russia-ties-poised-for-new-phase-of-trade-technology-and-industrial-cooperation-piyush-goyal/articleshow/134023686.cms)

**【2026-09-09】Government panel recommends retaining doorstep medicine delivery rule (GSR 220(E)), urges stricter enforcement instead of withdrawal** — A government sub-committee has recommended keeping the 2020 notification allowing doorstep delivery of medicines, saying that scrapping it would adversely affect elderly patients, those with chronic diseases, and people in rural and remote areas who depend on home delivery for regular medication. The matter was first deliberated by the Drugs Technical Advisory Board in April 2025, which constituted the sub-committee; the panel submitted its report in May and concluded that withdrawal is "not advisable." It instead recommended stricter and more effective implementation of existing provisions to prevent irregular or unauthorised online sale of drugs without valid prescriptions. The recommendation is a setback for the All India Organisation of Chemists and Druggists (AIOCD), which had sought withdrawal, alleging misuse by digital platforms including Swiggy to bypass Rule 65(e) of the Drugs Rules, 1945 on sale against original prescription. — Source: [Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/industry/healthcare/biotech/pharmaceuticals/government-panel-plans-to-retain-rule-allowing-doorstep-drug-delivery/articleshow/133939426.cms)

**【2026-09-09】Supreme Court reserves order; Centre to set up 3-member panel on a statutory framework for pharma marketing ethics** — The Supreme Court reserved its order on pleas seeking statutory regulation of pharmaceutical marketing practices. Solicitor General Tushar Mehta told the court that, after deliberations among the Department of Pharmaceuticals, the Department of Health and Family Welfare and the Department of Legal Affairs, the government has decided to constitute a three-member committee to examine "whether and, if yes, what can be the statutory framework" to ensure pharmaceutical companies do not offer unethical inducements to doctors; the committee has been asked to report within two months for the court's approval. Until then, the Uniform Code for Pharmaceutical Marketing Practices (UCPMP), 2024, will continue to govern the field. Petitioners (Federation of Medical and Sales Representatives Associations of India and others) highlighted the regulatory asymmetry: doctors face penalties under the Indian Medical Council (Professional Conduct, Etiquette and Ethics) Regulations, 2002 — including removal from the medical register for three months to over a year depending on the value received — while the pharmaceutical company offering the inducement faces no statutory sanction. The court is due to hear other matters relating to generic medicines on 22 September. — Source: [Economic Times LegalWorld](https://legal.economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/litigation/panel-being-set-up-to-suggest-framework-for-regulating-unethical-practices-of-pharma-firms-centre-to-sc/133939278)

**【2026-09-10】LUPIN global CFO: proposed 100–200% US tariffs on generics could raise US prices or cause shortages; flags India's cost disadvantage versus China** — Ramesh Swaminathan, Executive Director, Global CFO and Head of the API Plus SBU at Lupin, said proposed 100–200% US tariffs on generic drugs could push up medicine prices for US patients or lead to shortages if manufacturers cannot absorb costs — "either way, I think the consumer will be hurt." He said shifting generic production to the US is not commercially viable without firm commitments on volumes and prices plus possible government support. The tariff timeline: no duty for two years from 1 August 2026, rising to 100% in August 2028 and 200% in August 2029. Lupin is not currently affected by the Section 232 tariffs (up to 100% on imported patented pharmaceuticals and APIs) since its US-sold products are manufactured locally, and will continue to invest in its Somerset and Coral Springs units where economically feasible. Swaminathan said the Indian industry must overcome a cost and scale disadvantage against China, particularly in APIs, with logistics costs at least 14–15% higher and electricity more expensive than in China; he noted China now accounts for around 50% of global investigational new drug (IND) filings. Lupin will move up the value chain via complex generics, specialty medicines and biosimilars, and sees long-term GLP-1 potential (partnered with Zydus Lifesciences for generic semaglutide in India and with a Chinese company for the fortnightly GLP-1 injection Bofanglutide). — Source: [Financial Express](https://www.financialexpress.com/business/news-tariffs-may-raise-us-drug-prices-manufacturing-shift-not-easy-lupin-global-cfo-4335375/)

## 4. Risk Alerts

- **Unified licensing and inspection standards for sterile drugs in India will raise quality-system expectations on upstream suppliers**: The Centre's plan to bring all sterile drug products under CLAA licensing coincides with dense USFDA inspection activity at Indian sterile facilities (Indoco Goa Plant II: 7 observations in a Form 483; Eugia Telangana Unit 1: warning letter on aseptic processing). Indian customers are likely to tighten on-site audits of API, excipient and packaging suppliers and demand stronger elemental impurities/sterility assurance and data-integrity documentation — Chinese suppliers to Indian sterile dosage manufacturers should pre-align GMP and change-notification capabilities.
- **Rupee weakness plus high oil prices squeeze Indian customers' pricing and payment room for China-sourced purchases**: As of the morning of 11 September, USD/INR was about 95.64 (versus 95.09 on 10 September), with Brent crude futures around US$107/bbl. For an Indian pharma industry heavily dependent on imported APIs, intermediates and KSMs, rupee depreciation directly raises local-currency input costs while high oil prices lift solvent, packaging and logistics expenses. Buyers will press harder on price and seek longer payment terms in the near term, so quotations and order pacing for China-origin APIs/intermediates need reassessment.

- **India–Russia BIT negotiations and the pharma export corridor**: progress on local currency settlement mechanisms and its impact on Indian exporters to Russia (and related upstream raw material flows).
- **Demerger of Laurus Labs' subsidiary Laurus Synthesis**: outcome of the NCLT hearing held on 10 September and its impact on the company's CDMO structure and capital allocation.
