## 1. Government Pharma Policy

**【2026-09-17】India imposes final anti-dumping duties on Ethambutol Hydrochloride from China: Wuhan Wuyao at US$5,347/t, other Chinese producers at US$7,234/t** — India's Directorate General of Trade Remedies (DGTR) issued affirmative final findings on 17 September, recommending five-year anti-dumping duties on Ethambutol Hydrochloride (a first-line anti-TB API) originating in or exported from China and Thailand: **Wuhan Wuyao Pharmaceutical US$5,347/t**, **other Chinese producers US$7,234/t**, all Thai producers US$6,584/t; Indian customs HS codes **29051410 and 29051490**. The case was filed by **Lupin Limited** (initiated 2025-09-23). Versus the preliminary findings of 2026-01-30 (Wuhan Wuyao 5,124 / other China 6,096 / Thailand 6,513), **the rate for non-cooperating producers rose 18.7% (6,096→7,234)**, widening the cooperating/non-cooperating gap to US$1,887/t. China's total capacity is about 620 t/a; exports to India are small-batch, high-value — the duties will directly erode price competitiveness. — Source: [DGTR](https://dgtr.gov.in/) · [China Trade Remedy Information](https://www.163.com/dy/article/L73QNGVK0534A4SC.html)【Source: DGTR Final Findings 2026-09-17; HS 29051410 / 29051490】

**【2026-09-16】DGTR opens anti-circumvention probe on CPVC from China: checking transshipment via Malaysia, Japan and Thailand** — On 16 September DGTR initiated an anti-circumvention investigation into Chlorinated Polyvinyl Chloride (CPVC) from China and Korea RP, examining whether Chinese CPVC is transshipped to India via **Malaysia's Sasia Chlorine Polymers, Japan's EBC Corporation and Thailand's Sekisui Specialty Chemicals (Thailand)** to evade existing anti-dumping duties; **the probe covers only these three exporters**. Current duties: Chinese CPVC resin **US$790/t**, compounds **US$605/t** (Korea resin 593 / compounds 792). HS codes 39049010 and 39049090. Applicants include DCW Limited and Epigral Limited. **Signal: India's trade remedies are shifting from "origin control" to "route control"** — compliance risk for third-country re-labelling is rising fast. — Source: [DGTR](https://dgtr.gov.in/)【Source: DGTR Anti-Circumvention Initiation 2026-09-16】

**【2026-09-15】DGTR sunset review final findings: Sodium Hydrosulphite duty of US$440/t extended for another 5 years** — On 15 September DGTR concluded its sunset review, continuing the anti-dumping duty of **US$440/t** on Sodium Hydrosulphite originating in China for five more years; HS codes 28311010 and 28321020. China is the world's largest producer with capacity exceeding 800,000 t/a and India is a key export market — **the completed sunset review defeats the "wait five years" strategy**. — Source: [DGTR](https://dgtr.gov.in/)【Source: DGTR SSR Final Findings 2026-09-15】

**【2026-09-15】DGTR publishes list of interested parties in anti-dumping case on Dialyzers from China** — On 15 September DGTR published the registered list of interested parties in the anti-dumping investigation into **Dialyzers** originating in China; the case moves into disclosure and hearing preparation. **Relevance to us**: downstream customers exporting related medical consumables to India should watch knock-on effects on medical polymers and ancillary raw materials. — Source: [DGTR](https://dgtr.gov.in/)【Source: DGTR Interested Parties List 2026-09-15】

**【2026-09-17】Indian Pharmacopoeia Commission launches 6th National Pharmacovigilance Week (17–23 Sep), targeting at least one ADR monitoring centre per Aspirational District** — PIB release dated 17 September: the 6th National Pharmacovigilance Week (NPW) was inaugurated at the Indian Pharmacopoeia Commission (IPC) in Ghaziabad under the theme "Promoting Rational Use of Medicines through Pharmacovigilance". IPC, as the National Coordination Centre for the Pharmacovigilance Programme of India (NCC-PvPI), aims for **at least one ADR Monitoring Centre (AMC) and one Medical Device Monitoring Centre (MDMC) in every Aspirational District**, and released PvPI Newsletter Vol. 16, Issue 2. **Implication for suppliers: with pharmacovigilance reaching the grassroots, batch-to-batch consistency and impurity-profile expectations for APIs are likely to tighten further.** — Source: [PIB](https://pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2311715)【Source: PIB PRID 2311715 (2026-09-17)】

## 4. Risk Alerts

- **China-related trade remedies enter a "three-instrument" phase** — Within three days (15–17 Sep) DGTR issued the **Ethambutol HCl original final findings** (US$7,234/t for non-cooperating producers), **initiated an anti-circumvention probe on CPVC** (locking onto three transshippers in Malaysia, Japan and Thailand) and **concluded the Sodium Hydrosulphite sunset review** (US$440/t for another five years). With original investigations, anti-circumvention and sunset reviews combined, three old assumptions fail at once: that cooperating lowers duties, that waiting five years brings relief, and that third-country transshipment avoids duties.
- **The cooperating/non-cooperating duty gap keeps widening** — In the Ethambutol case Wuhan Wuyao received US$5,347/t while other Chinese producers face US$7,234/t — **a gap of US$1,887/t, with the non-cooperating rate up 18.7% from the preliminary stage**. For products where India matters, **cooperating has moved from optional to mandatory**.
- **India's fermentation-based API/CDMO capacity accelerates, front-loading China–India competition** — GTBL's acquisition of MBJ adds fermentation and precision fermentation (P450), plasmid DNA, ADC conjugation and recombinant protein platforms with plans to "reduce costs at Indian scale"; Merck is localising biological buffers in Bengaluru to cut import dependence; this follows Neuland's peptide module (planned to 18,000 L). Substitution pressure and customer stickiness are rising in **fermentation-based bulk APIs, high-end intermediates and peptides**.
- **Quality and compliance pressure rises on both ends** — Regulatory tightening on one side (Shilpa Medicare's Hyderabad analytical services unit closed with one procedural observation), and the deepening of India's pharmacovigilance network on the other (AMC and MDMC planned for every Aspirational District). **API and intermediate suppliers to Indian customers should expect stricter CoA, batch traceability and impurity-profile requirements.**
- **Channel compliance costs are rising** — The Health Ministry's draft would mandate CCTV in pharmacies with three months of recordings (G.S.R. 791(E)); AIOCD formally opposes it and argues government should bear the cost. Together with heavy enforcement against counterfeit drugs, requirements on **batch traceability, authorised-channel documentation and anti-counterfeiting** will keep increasing.
- **FX and energy costs** — The rupee stands at **1 USD ≈ ₹95.89** (real-time, 20 Sep), down about 6% against the dollar year-to-date. Strait of Hormuz tanker traffic hit a six-month high, easing oil-price risk, but India's pharma industry remains heavily dependent on imported APIs, intermediates and KSMs, so local-currency cost pressure persists and buyers continue to press for **price cuts, longer payment terms and FX hedging**.
