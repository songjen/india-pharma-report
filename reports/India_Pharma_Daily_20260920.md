## 1. Government Pharma Policy

**【2026-09-18】DoP says major regulatory reforms coming in 1–2 months; 12 companies bid for CDSCO's new digital drug regulatory system, rollout next month** — Speaking at the 8th CII Pharma & Lifesciences Summit in New Delhi on Sept 18, Department of Pharmaceuticals Secretary Manoj Joshi said "in the next one or two months we will be making a lot of changes in the regulatory system". The reform agenda spans the **Review Committee on Genetic Manipulation (RCGM) approval process, state regulatory approvals, faster turnaround on industry queries and testing across institutions**, and a shift from sequential to **parallel processing of approvals**, including allowing manufacturing without repeated inspections where these are not required. He urged industry to "keep pressurising us so that we carry out the reforms", citing the US and China where stronger links between industry and public research institutions have supported innovation, and said India under-uses the laboratories of the Department of Biotechnology (DBT) and CSIR. Drugs Controller General (India) Dr Rajeev Singh Raghuvanshi disclosed that **12 companies have bid for CDSCO's new Digital Drug Regulatory System (DDRS); technical presentations are complete and financial bids will be opened within 7–10 days, with the platform expected to be rolled out within a month**. The platform will use AI and data analytics to determine where regulatory intervention is needed and where it can be avoided; CDSCO has also constituted an **expert committee of AI specialists** to advise on evaluation, approval and regulation of AI-based healthcare products. Other developments: **more than 99% of India's regulatory processes are now fully digital**; CDSCO plans an **internal scientific cadre with 1,500 new positions agreed in principle** (recruitment after approval), targeting internal handling of 90–95%+ of Subject Expert Committee (SEC) work; **RCGM reforms** are expected to be resolved soon along with guidance on similar biologics; on the clinical-trial ecosystem, India may allow **first-in-human (FIH) studies for assets developed outside the country**, with global clinical trials in India rising from about 120–130 previously to around **160 expected this year**; and **about half of the bioequivalence (BE) studies conducted in India — particularly those for foreign clients — no longer require prior permission**, allowing sponsors to design their own protocols. — Source: [The Hindu BusinessLine](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/news/12-companies-bid-for-cdscos-new-digital-drug-regulatory-system-rollout-due-next-month/article71481262.ece) · [Indian Pharma Post](https://www.indianpharmapost.com/policy/govt-wants-industry-to-push-harder-for-faster-pharma-regulatory-reforms-manoj-joshi-21916)

**【2026-09-18】(Update) Chemists' body AIOCD formally opposes mandatory CCTV at medical stores** — The All India Organisation of Chemists and Druggists (AIOCD), representing **more than 1.2 million drug retailers**, has written to the Health Secretary opposing the government's proposal to make CCTV cameras mandatory at medical stores to track prescription drug sales, calling for a "practical and proportionate mechanism" instead of a blanket imposition. The body said the mandate would entail an additional financial burden of about **₹50,000–1,00,000 (roughly US$520–1,040) per store**, and flagged the practical difficulty of ensuring uninterrupted surveillance in rural and remote areas — "power outages, poor internet connectivity and non-availability of trained technicians". AIOCD said "a uniform requirement cannot reasonably be imposed on every medical shop", and that if such a mechanism is considered essential for public safety and regulatory monitoring, **the government should provide the necessary infrastructure and bear the maintenance costs**. The Health Ministry's draft notification proposes mandatory CCTV surveillance at all medical stores dispensing prescription drugs, with **footage retained for the previous three months**; stores dispensing prescription drugs are currently required to maintain written records of sales, particularly for Schedules H, H1 and X. — Source: [ET Pharma](https://pharma.economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/pharma-industry/chemists-body-oppose-centres-cctv-proposal-for-medical-stores/134324664)

## 2. Pharma Industry News

**【2026-09-18】Gujarat Themis Biosyn completes JPY 21.5 billion acquisition of Japan's MicroBiopharm, accelerating shift to a fermentation-based integrated CDMO** — Gujarat Themis Biosyn Limited (GTBL) has completed the acquisition of **100% equity of MicroBiopharm Japan Co., Ltd. (MBJ)** through Themis Biosyn Japan, its wholly owned subsidiary in Japan, for a total consideration of **JPY 21.5 billion (about ₹1,200 crore, roughly US$125 million)**, funded through a mix of debt and equity and acquired from funds managed or advised by T Capital Partners, a Japan-based private equity fund. First announced on **2026-05-22**, the transaction closed after receipt of regulatory approvals and satisfaction of closing conditions; it is expected to be EPS accretive and MBJ's existing management team will continue to lead operations. MBJ has **over 60 years of experience**, operates **three GMP-compliant manufacturing plants** with an FDA and PMDA inspection track record, and recorded **FY26 revenue of JPY 9.5 billion**, with a business mix spanning proprietary APIs/intermediates, CDMO, CDMO Bio and developed pharmaceuticals (biologics) plus non-pharma. **Around 40% of MBJ's revenue comes from outside Japan**, and its top six customers have been associated with the company for more than two decades. Through the deal GTBL enters **precision fermentation via MBJ's proprietary P450 enzyme library for hydroxylation**, complementing its existing fermentation strengths and reducing reliance on traditional chemical routes. MBJ will supply **APIs and intermediates for oncology, immunosuppressants, peptides and antibiotics** to global pharmaceutical companies including Big Pharma. GTBL also gains access to **plasmid DNA, ADC conjugation and recombinant protein technologies**, plus an integrated CDMO platform from discovery through commercial manufacturing — and expects to leverage **its India manufacturing scale to improve cost competitiveness** on selected high-value products. Deloitte India acted as exclusive financial adviser, Tokyo International Law Office (TKI) as legal counsel, and Avendus Capital as exclusive financing arranger. — Source: [Express Pharma](https://www.expresspharma.in/gujarat-themis-biosyn-completes-jpy-21-5-billion-acquisition-of-microbiopharm-japan/) · [Indian Pharma Post](https://www.indianpharmapost.com/biopharma/gujarat-themis-biosyn-completes-rs-1200-crore-acquisition-of-japans-microbiopharm-21919)

**【2026-09-18】Merck KGaA invests €13 million to expand Bengaluru (Jigani) site, adding biological buffer capacity to cut import dependence** — Germany's Merck (known as Merck in India) is investing about **€13 million (over ₹140 crore, roughly US$14.6 million)** to expand its flagship manufacturing facility in **Jigani, Bengaluru**, adding **biological buffer manufacturing** to its established chemistry capabilities so that it can serve customers from R&D and scale-up through commercial production. Operational since **2003** on a **10-acre** parcel, the site houses capabilities in specialty and fine chemicals, custom synthesis and catalogue product manufacturing, plus a Microbiology Application and Training Lab, a **stable isotopes manufacturing plant established with the Heavy Water Board under the Department of Atomic Energy**, and a **7,200 sq m temperature-controlled distribution centre**. The new capacity will manufacture biological buffers and raw materials for diagnostic applications used in DNA research, cell culture, diagnostics and drug manufacturing, produced locally under temperature-controlled conditions to **improve supply reliability and reduce import dependence**. Dhananjay Singh, MD of Merck's Life Science business in India, said India is emerging as an important hub for life science manufacturing and that the investment reinforces Merck's "in-region, for-region" approach; DPIIT Secretary Amardeep Singh Bhatia and Alexandre Callegaro, Head of the Department for Economic and Global Affairs at the German Embassy in New Delhi, also commented. — Source: [ET Pharma](https://pharma.economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/pharma-industry/merck-channels-over-140-crore-into-bengaluru-manufacturing-site-expansion/134340572) · [Express Pharma](https://www.expresspharma.in/merck-expands-jigani-facility-to-boost-life-science-manufacturing-in-india/)

## 3. Key Client Updates

**【2026-09-18】ZYDUS LIFESCIENCES: Novartis halts ALS candidate VHB937; Bernstein lifts target price to ₹1,457 (28% upside)** — Global brokerage Bernstein maintained its "Outperform" rating on Zydus Lifesciences and raised its target price to **₹1,457 per share (about US$15.2)**, implying **more than 28% upside** from the Sept 17 close of ₹1,135.10 — the highest target among the 34 analysts tracking the stock (the consensus implies only about 8% upside). The key catalyst is **Novartis' Sept 16 decision to discontinue its ALS candidate VHB937 (lifonebart)**, which **failed to meet primary and secondary endpoints** in a Phase II trial of 251 patients with early-stage ALS. Bernstein said this **removes a direct competitor** to Zydus' oral ALS drug Usnoflast, a novel chemical entity described by the company as an **oral NLRP3 inflammasome inhibitor** that targets a different biological pathway. Usnoflast has US FDA clearance to run Phase II(b) trials and holds **Orphan Drug Designation for ALS in the US**. Bernstein said the key value driver is whether the ongoing **UNITE-ALS study** shows a clinically meaningful slowing of disease progression; if results are positive, its oral dosing and applicability across a broad ALS population could support an **annual revenue run-rate of around US$100 million**, with faster ramp-up than Zydus' Saroglitazar as the company's second high-return US new chemical entity. Bernstein also cited the potential for FDA priority review. Zydus is additionally developing Usnoflast across **Parkinson's disease, ulcerative colitis and cryopyrin-associated periodic syndromes**. Zydus shares gained about 2.9% on Sept 18, touching an intraday high of ₹1,167.3, and are up about 27% year to date. — Source: [CNBC TV18](https://www.cnbctv18.com/market/zydus-lifesciences-share-price-bernstein-says-buy-novartis-unsoflat-als-usfda-target-upside-19993452.htm)

## 4. Risk Alerts

- **A regulatory "acceleration window" is opening, but filing and change-control requirements may reset in parallel** — The DoP has said reforms (RCGM, parallel processing, removal of repeat inspections) will land within 1–2 months, and CDSCO's new DDRS platform should go live within about a month. Faster approvals are positive for suppliers, but platform transitions typically come with adjusted filing formats, templates and data requirements; pending registrations, variations and BE programmes should build in buffer and maintain close alignment with clients' RA teams.
- **India's fermentation-based API and CDMO capacity is accelerating, bringing China–India competition forward** — Three capability investments landed this week: GTBL gains fermentation/precision fermentation (P450 enzyme library), plasmid DNA, ADC conjugation and recombinant proteins via MBJ and plans to use "India scale" to cut costs; Merck is localising biological buffers and other raw materials in Bengaluru to **reduce import dependence**; and these follow Neuland's peptide module going on stream (planned up to 18,000 L) and Amneal's Gujarat peptide site. Substitution pressure and client stickiness in **fermentation-based bulk APIs, high-end intermediates and peptides** are rising together.
- **India's trade-remedy actions against China remain high-density** — Beyond the concluded **affirmative final anti-dumping determination on Ethambutol Hydrochloride** (US$7,234/t for all other Chinese producers, above Thailand's US$6,584/t), the Commerce Ministry has recently pushed a copper data cables final determination, a **CPVC anti-circumvention investigation**, and a sunset-review final determination on sodium hydrosulphite. Pharma-related cases (ethambutol, the dialyzers anti-dumping investigation) warrant close tracking of the duty notification and effective date, plus assessment of transshipment and third-country routing compliance risk.
- **Channel compliance costs are rising as retailer resistance becomes explicit** — The Health Ministry proposes mandatory CCTV at medical stores with three months of retained footage, and AIOCD has formally objected, arguing government should fund infrastructure and maintenance. Combined with high enforcement pressure from the counterfeit-drug cases, India's requirements on **batch traceability, authorised-channel proof and anti-counterfeiting (including QR codes)** will keep escalating; API and intermediate suppliers to Indian clients should expect stricter CoA, batch and logistics-chain documentation, and should guard against channel inventory pressure being pushed upstream.
- **Currency and energy costs** — The rupee stands at 1 USD ≈ ₹95.99 (benchmark 95.99 on Sept 19) with Brent crude still elevated. India's pharma industry depends heavily on imported APIs, intermediates and KSMs, so local-currency cost pressure persists and buyers' demands for **price cuts, longer payment terms and FX hedging** continue.
