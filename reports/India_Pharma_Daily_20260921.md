## 1. Government Pharma Policy

**【2026-09-15】India plans to extend the BA/BE prior-intimation regime to domestic registrations; Form CT-17 import licence processing time to be halved from 90 to 45 working days** — The government proposes to simplify the regulatory process for bioavailability/bioequivalence (BA/BE) studies used for **domestic drug approvals**, allowing eligible applicants to start studies **after merely intimating the regulator instead of obtaining prior permission** (the prior-intimation mechanism so far applied only to export-oriented BA/BE studies). In parallel, it plans to **halve the processing timeline for Form CT-17 import licences** (new drugs and reference products for analytical and non-clinical testing) under the New Drugs and Clinical Trials Rules 2019 **from 90 to 45 working days**, and to review Rules 67, 68, 69 and 70 to improve ease of doing business. The proposal has been placed before the **Drugs Technical Advisory Board (DTAB), scheduled to meet this month**. **High-risk categories explicitly excluded**: sex hormones, cytotoxic drugs, **beta-lactam antibiotics**, vaccines, cell and gene therapy products, biologics containing live microorganisms, and narcotics and psychotropic drugs — these continue to require a formal licence. — Source: [ET Pharma](https://pharma.economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/policy-and-regulations/centre-proposes-easier-norms-for-faster-drug-rollout/134249981)

**【2026-09-16】Supreme Court receives PIL seeking 3-month probe and 1-year trial timelines for fake drug cases, plus mandatory videography of searches and sampling** — Lawyer Ashwini Upadhyay has filed a Public Interest Litigation (PIL) in the Supreme Court, naming the Ministries of Health & Family Welfare, Law & Justice and Women & Child Development, all states and union territories, and the Law Commission as respondents. The plea seeks: (1) completion of **investigations within three months and trials within one year** in fake medicine cases; (2) standard operating procedures for **search, seizure and sampling** and **time-bound forensic examination**; (3) **mandatory digital recording and videography of search, seizure, sampling and inventory proceedings**; and (4) a **Law Commission report on legislative changes** together with a **graded sentencing policy**. Citing the Bengaluru fake-cancer-drug case and cough-syrup deaths, the plea argues that 85 years after the Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940, counterfeit production and sale continue to rise, and that the existing framework has a "vacuum" — no uniform investigation procedure, no fast-track mechanism and no effective financial investigation. — Source: [ET Pharma](https://pharma.economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/policy-and-regulations/pil-in-sc-seeks-time-bound-probe-stringent-action-against-fake-drug-networks/134286694)

**【2026-09-17】(Update) DGTR issues corrigendum to the initiation notice in the anti-circumvention probe on CPVC from China PR and Korea RP (provisional assessment)** — On 17 September, the Directorate General of Trade Remedies (DGTR), Ministry of Commerce & Industry, issued a **"Corrigendum to the Initiation Notification regarding Provisional Assessment"** in the **anti-circumvention investigation into Chlorinated Polyvinyl Chloride (CPVC)** from China PR and Korea RP, initiated on 16 September, which examines transhipment through three overseas exporters — **Sasia Chlorine Polymers (Malaysia), EBC Corporation (Japan) and Sekisui Specialty Chemicals (Thailand)** — to determine whether existing anti-dumping duties are being circumvented. Current duty rates: Chinese CPVC resin **USD 790/tonne** and compound **USD 605/tonne** (Korean resin 593, compound 792); HS codes 39049010, 39049090. **Implication: India's scrutiny of third-country transhipment routes has moved from initiation into the provisional-assessment stage, raising compliance cost and working-capital exposure for re-routing via South-East and East Asia.** — Source: [DGTR](https://www.dgtr.gov.in/)【Source: DGTR Corrigendum to the Initiation Notification regarding Provisional Assessment 2026-09-17】

## 2. Pharma Industry News

**【2026-09-20】Karnataka FDA cancels 16 drug licences and suspends 8 others, including a Pfizer C&F depot; seized value revised to ₹5.05 crore** — The Karnataka Food Safety and Drug Administration (FDA) announced on 20 September that it has **cancelled the drug licences of 16 wholesale/retail establishments and suspended eight others**, including the **Carrying & Forwarding (C&F) depot of Pfizer Ltd at Kempalinganahalli, Nelamangala taluk, Bengaluru Rural district**. Of the 24 establishments under scrutiny, 22 are in Bengaluru Urban and Rural districts, with one each in Hubballi and Belagavi. The case stems from a **18 August** raid on a farmhouse in Bidadi, where counterfeit medicines, an injection-filling machine and forged labels and stamps were seized, with the **inventory now valued at ₹5.05 crore (about USD 5.3 million)** — including 5,640 vials of CEFIAVIGENT 2.5 Injection (₹3.38 crore) and 1,531 vials of HIZLAM Injectables (₹52.24 lakh). The alleged modus operandi was to **procure cheaper medicines from neighbouring states, refill them into new vials and relabel them as expensive MNC life-saving brands**, with **forged labels carrying QR/scanning codes that were found completely non-functional on verification**. The FDA has **blocked the batch numbers and entries of the seized drugs on its portal** to prevent further distribution, classified the products as spurious under Section 17B of the Drugs and Cosmetics Act (offences under Section 27), and initiated criminal proceedings. The state health minister said **Pfizer had not cooperated** and that links in other states are being traced. — Source: [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/india/karnataka/karnataka-cancels-drug-licences-of-16-retailers-wholesalers-over-spurious-medicines-4153077) · [The South First](https://thesouthfirst.com/karnataka/counterfeit-drug-racket-karnataka-fda-cancels-licences-of-16-firms-suspends-eight-others-including-pfizer)【Source: Karnataka FDA Commissioner's statement 2026-09-20】

**【2026-09-20】US weighs allowing most pharma licensing deals with China; Treasury-drafted rules could let US firms invest in Chinese drug candidates** — According to Reuters, the US Treasury is drafting rules that would **likely preserve the ability of US pharmaceutical companies to strike most licensing deals for Chinese drugs** — a departure from the administration's tightening of business with China in semiconductors and electric vehicles, and **looser than restrictions sought by some lawmakers**. The rules would likely allow US pharma companies to invest in promising new drugs developed by Chinese companies **as long as they are not related to pathogens or biotechnology that could be weaponized**; they are not yet final. Key figures: **out-licensing deals in Chinese biotech were worth USD 115 billion last year** (GlobalData), and **almost half of US deals to bring in licensed drugs from abroad in 2025 were with Chinese companies**. Landmark transactions include **Bristol Myers Squibb's partnership with Jiangsu Hengrui worth up to USD 15.2 billion** and **Pfizer's collaboration with Innovent Biologics worth up to USD 10.5 billion across 12 oncology programmes**. Pfizer CEO Albert Bourla said licensing Chinese medicines does not create a national security concern and has discussed the issue with the Treasury Secretary, the Secretary of State and HHS officials; opponents include Ginkgo Bioworks CEO Jason Kelly and lawmakers John Moolenaar and Debbie Dingell, who are co-sponsoring a bill to tighten Treasury regulation of biotechnology-related investments, licensing and joint ventures. Sources said **Treasury is unlikely to unveil the rules before the Xi–Trump meeting this week**. — Source: [ET Pharma (Reuters)](https://pharma.economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/pharma-industry/us-weighs-allowing-most-pharma-licensing-deals-with-china-sources-say/134370190)

**【2026-09-15】USFDA issues Form 483 with seven observations to Piramal Pharma's Morpeth (UK) facility over practices, procedures and documentation** — Piramal Pharma disclosed to the exchanges on **14 September** that the USFDA conducted a GMP inspection of its **Morpeth facility in the United Kingdom** from **3 to 11 September 2026**; the inspection concluded with a **Form 483 containing seven observations**, which the company characterised as relating to enhancements in its practices, procedures and documentation. The company said it will address all seven observations and submit a detailed response within the stipulated timeline; a Form 483 is not a final FDA determination on GMP compliance. **The site is a core Piramal Pharma Solutions asset producing both APIs and oral solid doses, with about 84% of revenue earned from regulated markets (US, Europe, Japan)**. It is the company's second significant Form 483 event in roughly a year (four observations at its Kentucky site in December 2025; a quality-control-related Form 483 at a Maharashtra API plant in February 2025). — Source: [CNBC TV18](https://www.cnbctv18.com/market/piramal-pharma-share-price-fall-usfda-form-483-morpeth-uk-facility-gmp-inspection-7-observations-19990884.htm)【Source: Company exchange disclosure 2026-09-14】

**【2026-09-16】Shilpa Biologicals signs exclusive MENA licence with SPIMACO Bio for a PD-1 biosimilar** — **Shilpa Biologicals**, the integrated biologics development and manufacturing organisation of the Shilpa Medicare group, announced a strategic licensing agreement with **SPIMACO Bio**, part of Saudi Arabia's SPIMACO group, covering **exclusive commercialisation of a PD-1 biosimilar in the Middle East and North Africa (MENA)**. **Shilpa retains the intellectual property and manufacturing**, while SPIMACO Bio handles registration and commercialisation in the region. **Implication: Indian biologics CDMOs are going global on an "IP and manufacturing at home, regional partner for registration and commercialisation" model — the same division of labour seen on the API side, where regulated-market filings are held in India and volume growth comes from emerging markets.** — Source: [Express Pharma](https://www.expresspharma.in/shilpa-biologicals-and-spimaco-bio-sign-strategic-license-agreement-for-pd-1-biosimilar-for-mena-market/) · [Indian Pharma Post](https://www.indianpharmapost.com/biopharma/shilpa-biologicals-spimaco-sign-mena-licensing-deal-for-pd-1-biosimilars-21860)

## 4. Risk Alerts

- **The fake-drug case has escalated from seizure to licence revocation, materially raising channel and traceability compliance requirements** — The Karnataka FDA cancelled 16 licences and suspended eight, including a Pfizer C&F depot, in a case marked by **"forged QR codes that failed verification"** and **"cheaper medicines procured from neighbouring states and refilled"**; in parallel, a Supreme Court PIL seeks **mandatory videography of search and sampling, time-bound forensics, and 3-month/1-year investigation and trial timelines**. For API and intermediate suppliers to India, the intensity of **batch blocking, authorised-channel documentation, CoA and logistics-chain verification** will increase; **the C&F warehousing link of multinational companies has now entered the enforcement perimeter**, so suppliers should review the authorisations and records of their own and their customers' warehouses.
- **Beta-lactam antibiotics are explicitly excluded from this round of facilitation** — Neither the extension of the BA/BE prior-intimation regime nor the halving of the CT-17 timeline applies to **beta-lactam antibiotics, cytotoxic drugs, sex hormones, vaccines, cell and gene therapy products, biologics with live microorganisms, or narcotics and psychotropics**. The cephalosporin/penicillin chain **continues to face higher registration and import hurdles and longer timelines in India**; suppliers of cephalosporin intermediates and APIs should not expect downstream customer registration cycles to shorten as a result of this round of deregulation.
- **Conflicting US policy signals are pushing up political risk for Indian pharma exports** — On one side, the US may ease restrictions on pharma licensing with China (USD 115 billion of out-licensing deals in 2025; almost half of US in-licensing from China); on the other, the **Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act of 2026 authorises tariffs of up to 100% on countries buying Russian oil**, leaving 45% of India's US-bound exports (including generics) in an "exempt or Section 232" grey zone and increasing pressure in the India–US trade negotiations. **At product level, generic formulations, biosimilars and generic APIs remain outside Section 232 for now, but the policy stance could shift around the 29 September effective date for "other companies"; export pricing and contractual tariff clauses should retain flexibility.**
- **Quality-compliance pressure remains undiminished** — Piramal Pharma's Morpeth (UK) CDMO site, which earns about 84% of revenue from regulated markets, received seven observations from a 3–11 September inspection; Apitoria's Unit-II received one procedural observation. Inspection density at Indian API/CDMO sites remains elevated, and **buyers are expected to tighten supplier audits, deviation management and change control requirements**.
- **Currency** — The rupee stands at **1 USD ≈ ₹95.97** (real-time, 21 September), slightly weaker than Friday's 95.89; combined with uncertainty over the US Russian-oil bill and the India–US trade negotiations, Indian buyers' pressure on **prices, payment terms and hedging** continues.
