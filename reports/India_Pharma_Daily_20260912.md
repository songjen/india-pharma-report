## 1. Government Pharma Policy

**【2026-09-10】Supreme Court refuses to entertain PIL challenging the national HPV vaccination programme** — The Supreme Court of India (CJI Surya Kant with Justices Joymalya Bagchi and V Mohana) declined to entertain a public interest litigation filed by the Universal Health Organisation questioning the implementation of the government's nationwide human papillomavirus (HPV) vaccination campaign for adolescent girls. The plea alleged violation of constitutional guarantees of informed consent, bodily autonomy, transparency and accountability, and sought an adverse-event reporting mechanism plus a vaccine-injury compensation framework. The Bench said that whenever there is a case of incorrect vaccination, ordinary rules of compensation will apply, and orally observed that the PIL was filed with an ulterior motive and lacked bona fide, finding no scientific basis linking reported deaths to the vaccine; the plea was subsequently withdrawn and dismissed as withdrawn. India launched the nationwide campaign on 28 February from Ajmer, Rajasthan, using single-dose quadrivalent Gardasil-4 covering HPV types 16/18 (cervical cancer) and 6/11. A separate petition by gynaecologists is pending before the Delhi High Court, listed next on 29 October. — Source: [The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/sci-tech/health/sc-refuses-to-entertain-pil-raising-concerns-over-hpv-vaccination-campaign/article71450584.ece)

**【2026-09-10】Kerala abolishes KMSCL quality control wing, shifts drug testing to State Drugs Control Department** — Kerala Health Minister K. Muraleedharan announced that the Quality Control wing of the Kerala Medical Services Corporation Limited (KMSCL) has been scrapped and that all medicines procured through KMSCL must henceforth be inspected and quality-tested by the State Drugs Control Department before distribution to government hospitals and other public institutions. The decision follows findings that medicines worth crores of rupees procured and distributed through KMSCL were substandard, and concerns that the same agency handling procurement was also testing the medicines it purchased, creating scope for corruption and irregularities. The Drugs Control Department holds the statutory authority, technical expertise and regulatory mandate for the task; given its staff shortage, the government has approved recruitment on temporary contract basis. The Minister has written to the Principal Secretary (Health) on strengthening the quality-assurance mechanism. — Source: [The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/kerala/drugs-quality-control-division-at-kmscl-dismantled/article71452179.ece)

**【2026-09-11】Indian Pharmacopoeia Commission releases IPRS reference substance for enoxaparin sodium bioassays** — The Indian Pharmacopoeia Commission (IPC) held a two-day National Conference on "Quality and Safety of Biosimilars: Strengthening India's Biopharma SHAKTI Vision" on 10–11 September at C-CAMP, Bengaluru, with 160 participants from regulators, industry and academia. Its concrete output was the release of an **Indian Pharmacopoeia Reference Substance (IPRS) for Enoxaparin Sodium for bioassays** — an authenticated, value-assigned reference material that lets laboratories measure biological activity on a common yardstick; such standards have previously often had to be imported. Technical sessions covered pharmacopoeial standards, critical quality attributes, comparability assessment, immunogenicity and pharmacovigilance, with the IPC pressing for alignment with evolving international expectations. — Source: [The Cavalier](https://www.cavalier.in/nda-current-affairs/2026-09-11/biosimilars-ipc-enoxaparin-reference-substance-2026)

## 4. Risk Alerts

- **US inspection activity on India is intense and mixed, raising upstream supplier audit requirements**: within a single day on 11 September, multiple outcomes were disclosed — three Form 483 observations at Strides Pharma's Chennai formulations plant (2–11 September), two observations from the USFDA records assessment at Dr. Reddy's Mexico API site, a VAI classification for Jubilant Pharmova's US sterile CMO facility, and zero observations at Alembic's Vadodara BE facility — spanning formulations, sterile, API and bioequivalence operations. Indian customers' on-site audits, data-integrity expectations and change-notification requirements for upstream API, intermediate and packaging suppliers will tighten in step; Chinese suppliers serving Indian formulation and sterile customers should prepare audit and quality documentation in advance.
- **Rupee still above 95.5 and Brent around US$105/bbl squeeze Indian buyers' pricing and payment room**: USD/INR was about 95.57 in the morning of 12 September, with Brent crude futures around US$105/bbl and WTI around US$100/bbl. For an Indian pharma industry heavily dependent on imported APIs, intermediates and KSMs, rupee weakness raises local-currency input costs directly, while high oil prices push up solvent, packaging and logistics costs; buyers' demands for price concessions and longer payment terms are likely to strengthen in near-term negotiations.

## 5. Tomorrow's Watchlist

- **Follow-up on USFDA inspections**: Strides Pharma's response to the three observations at its Chennai Alathur plant and whether it affects its US approval pipeline; Dr. Reddy's remediation of the Form FDA 2953 observations at its Mexico API site and implications for US supply scheduling (records-assessment observations do not usually trigger import restrictions directly).

- **Expansion of the IPC reference-standard system**: after the enoxaparin sodium IPRS for bioassays, whether it extends to more biosimilar products and low-molecular-weight heparins, changing the source of comparator standards for registration and release of related products in India.
