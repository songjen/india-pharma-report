## 1. Government Pharma Policy

**【2026-09-15】NPPA's limited powers block the parliamentary proposal for a 20% cap on medical device margins** — The Parliamentary Standing Committee on Chemicals and Fertilizers has recommended capping the gap between the landed cost and the MRP of medical devices at 20%, but the proposal faces a structural obstacle: under the Drugs (Prices Control) Order, 2013 (DPCO), the National Pharmaceutical Pricing Authority (NPPA) can directly fix prices only for "scheduled" drugs and devices. For non-scheduled products — including the IV infusion sets, catheters, syringes, nebulisers and oxygen masks at issue — manufacturers determine the initial price and NPPA can only restrict subsequent annual increases to 10%. A blanket margin cap would therefore require either a policy-level amendment to the DPCO or bringing specific products under Trade Margin Rationalisation (TMR). The issue gained urgency after a Maharashtra government survey found private hospitals procuring **IV infusion sets at about ₹11.05 and billing patients ₹325 (a markup of more than 2,800%)**, and catheters procured at about ₹29.41 and billed at ₹310, with markups reaching **2,841%** in some categories. Maharashtra FDA Commissioner Tukaram Mundhe wrote to the Department of Pharmaceuticals and NPPA on 10 August seeking a national review of device pricing and guidelines on the permissible gap between trade procurement cost and declared MRP; drug-control authorities in Punjab, Rajasthan and Tamil Nadu have made similar representations. NPPA is comparing company filings and internal price lists with the Maharashtra findings. The Standing Committee also noted that TMR has been applied only three times (select diabetic and cardiovascular medicines in 2014, 42 anti-cancer medicines in 2019, six categories of Covid-related devices in 2021) and called for a permanent TMR framework under the DPCO. — Source: [The Hindu BusinessLine](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/news/nppas-limited-powers-pose-hurdle-to-20-cap-on-medical-device-margins/article71468519.ece)

## 2. Pharma Industry News

**【2026-09-15】US Commerce Department issues final results of the anti-dumping administrative review on glycine from India: 57.17% weighted-average dumping margin for two exporters** — The US Department of Commerce (ITA) published the final results of the 2024-2025 anti-dumping duty administrative review on glycine from India, determining that the reviewed producers/exporters sold subject merchandise below normal value during the period of review (1 June 2024 to 31 May 2025). The **weighted-average dumping margin for both Medilane Healthcare Private Limited and Mulji Mehta Enterprises is 57.17%**. Commerce intends to issue assessment instructions to US Customs and Border Protection (CBP) no earlier than 35 days after publication, with cash deposit rates set at the above margins for future entries. Glycine is a widely used pharmaceutical and food ingredient (buffer, excipient and intermediate); the ruling directly raises the US cash cost for the affected Indian exporters. — Source: [Federal Register](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-09-15/pdf/2026-18787.pdf)

## 4. Risk Alerts

- **India's pricing-regulation "power boundary" dispute could spill over into the API and consumables chain**: NPPA's only tool for non-scheduled products is the 10% annual increase limit, while Parliament is pushing for a uniform margin cap and a permanent TMR framework. If TMR is extended to more categories, procurement pricing across Indian public and private channels would be reset, and suppliers of APIs, intermediates and packaging materials to India should assess channel pricing and annual price-increase headroom in advance.
- **The rupee has weakened further to 95.99, with Brent above USD 108/bbl — Indian customers' price pressure and payment-term demands continue**: USD/INR was about 95.99 in the morning of 16 September (about 95.55-95.61 a week earlier), a depreciation of about 6.8% against the dollar so far this year; elevated oil prices are pushing up solvent, packaging and logistics costs. For an Indian pharma industry highly dependent on imported APIs, intermediates and KSMs, the local-currency cost of imported raw materials continues to rise, keeping buyer pressure on prices and demands for longer payment terms strong in the near term.
- **Trade remedies are applying pressure in both directions**: on 15 September the US issued final results of its anti-dumping administrative review on glycine from India (57.17%), raising US cash costs for affected Indian exporters; at the same time, India-China trade talks have put the trade imbalance in chemicals and pharmaceutical intermediates back on the agenda. If Chinese API/intermediate exports to India face a new round of anti-dumping or anti-subsidy investigations, transhipment compliance and costs would rise in tandem.
- **Indian CDMO/API players are accelerating capital expenditure, moving competition in complex manufacturing forward**: Laurus has raised FY27 capex to ₹2,000 crore and is extending into peptides/ADCs/gene therapies, while Neuland's peptide capacity is planned at up to 18,000L with quarterly revenue up 116.3%. India's capacity and customer stickiness in outsourced manufacturing of "complex small molecules + peptides + ADCs" is rising quickly, and the "last mile" substitution pressure on Chinese intermediate suppliers at Indian customers will become increasingly visible.
