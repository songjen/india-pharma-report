## 1. Government Pharma Policy

**【2026-09-26】Finance Minister: incentives to build domestic manufacturing and end dependence; says India's bulk pharma exports to China actually move via third countries** — Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman said on 25 September that the Central Government has used budget announcements to support industry either in building long-term domestic manufacturing or in finding alternative sources at short notice, in order to remove dependence on other countries, and that those willing to exit such dependence are being incentivised. She said India's exports to China "actually go to China" but are not captured in the accounts because they move via Vietnam and other third countries, and that India does not get market access for bulk-value goods such as pharmaceutical generics. Recalling that India once led in APIs before losing out to low-price competition, she said incentives have now been given to restart domestic production. — Source: [The Hindu BusinessLine](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/economy/centre-extending-support-to-manufacturing-to-end-dependency-says-finance-minister/article71512441.ece)【Source: PTI report, updated 2026-09-26 16:19 IST; remarks delivered 2026-09-25】

**【2026-09-25】CDSCO: quality responsibility is end-to-end; outsourcing an activity does not outsource quality responsibility** — Speaking at the ETPharma Future-Ready Supply Chain Summit, Dr K Narendran, Deputy Drug Controller, CDSCO, said pharmaceutical quality does not end with manufacturing and must be preserved until the medicine reaches the patient, and that Good Distribution Practices (GDP) are a critical link between manufacturing quality and patient safety. Suppliers and customers must be qualified, medicines must move only through authorised channels, and "outsourcing the activity does not mean outsourcing responsibility for quality" — pharma companies and logistics partners must define temperature-control and deviation responsibilities through quality or technical agreements. Cold-chain and biological products and other high-value items warrant stricter, risk-based oversight. — Source: [ET Pharma](https://pharma.economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/supply-chain-and-logistics/pharma-supply-chains-need-end-to-end-quality-oversight-cdscos-dr-k-narendran/134479536)【Source: CDSCO Deputy Drug Controller remarks, 2026-09-25】

## 2. Pharma Industry News

**【2026-09-25】Pharmexcil: bilateral trade deals open doors, but competitiveness still hinges on ecosystem depth** — At the ETPharma Supply Chain Summit, Pharmexcil Chairman Namit Joshi said geopolitical instability has made bilateral trade agreements increasingly important, yet pharma has not always received sufficient prominence in the negotiations. He added that competitiveness is shifting from "what" companies manufacture to "how" they manufacture, with ESG, green chemistry and responsible manufacturing — for instance environmental compliance in antibiotic production — set to influence global tenders and market access. Sarvesh Singh, Director & CEO, Life Sciences Pharma Telangana, said agreements only provide the base and do not determine where investment goes; site selection still depends on infrastructure, costs, talent and regulatory predictability. — Source: [ET Pharma](https://pharma.economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/supply-chain-and-logistics/bilateral-trade-deals-can-open-doors-but-indias-pharma-scale-up-will-depend-on-ecosystem-depth/134482182)【Source: ETPharma Supply Chain Summit 2026 remarks, 2026-09-25】

**【2026-09-25】SUVEN Chairman: India must move from "pharmacy of the world" to an engine of drug discovery** — Venkateswarlu Jasti, Chairman & MD, SUVEN Life Sciences, told the ETPharma Summit that India's next opportunity lies not in generics and contract manufacturing but in original drug discovery and collaborative research. His company moved from commodity manufacturing to speciality chemistry and contract research and manufacturing, and then into CNS drug discovery. He stressed that innovation is a 10-to-15-year commitment; for supply-chain partners, the key is to deliver on time and flag problems early so that customers can prepare alternatives. — Source: [ET Pharma](https://pharma.economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/supply-chain-and-logistics/indias-pharma-story-must-move-beyond-generics-to-innovation-suvens-venkat-jasti/134482720)【Source: Company management remarks, 2026-09-25】

**【2026-09-25】IOL Chemicals obtains EU-REACH registration for Triacetin** — Indian API manufacturer IOL Chemicals and Pharmaceuticals announced that its product Triacetin has obtained EU-REACH registration from the European Chemicals Agency (ECHA), meeting the mandatory environmental and health compliance benchmarks under the EU chemicals framework. Triacetin is used mainly as a solvent, plasticiser, humectant, formulation aid and flavouring agent, and is not an active drug substance. — Source: [Indian Pharma Post](https://www.indianpharmapost.com/news/briefs-iol-chemicals-and-pharmaceuticals-and-dr-lal-pathlabs-21966)【Source: Company announcement, 2026-09-25】

## 3. Key Client Updates

**【2026-09-26】ZYDUS LIFESCIENCES: USFDA inspection of US office pharmacovigilance system closes with nil observations** — The company disclosed that a USFDA inspection of its pharmacovigilance and post-marketing surveillance system at its New Jersey, US office, conducted from 22 to 25 September 2026, concluded with nil observations. The disclosure was made under Regulation 30 of the SEBI (Listing Obligations and Disclosure Requirements) Regulations, 2015. — Source: [CNBC TV18](https://www.tradingview.com/news/cnbctv:f1132e0f0094b:0)【Source: Zydus Lifesciences exchange filing, 2026-09-26】

**【2026-09-26】SUN PHARMA: voluntary US recall of Lidocaine ointment and Nystatin cream** — The company is voluntarily recalling Lidocaine Ointment USP 5% (65,328 jars, lot AD48154, expiry 2027-04-30, after failing assay specifications at the 24-month long-term stability station) and Nystatin Cream USP (15 g tubes, lots AD49605/AD49606, expiry 2027-05-31, after failing stability specifications) in the United States. Both are classified by the FDA as Class II recalls; they were manufactured at Taro's Brampton (Canada) and Haifa Bay (Israel) facilities respectively. — Source: [ET Pharma](https://pharma.economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/pharma-industry/sun-pharma-recalls-topical-anesthetic-anti-fungal-cream-in-us/134495434)【Source: USFDA enforcement report, recall numbers D-0848-2026 / D-0872-2026】
