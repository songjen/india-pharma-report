## 1. Government Pharma Policy

**【2026-09-12】BRICS New Delhi Declaration reaches multiple understandings on health and medical product regulation cooperation** — The BRICS Leaders' Summit issued the New Delhi Declaration, in which health and medical products regulation cooperation forms one of the substantive clusters: (i) welcoming the establishment of a dedicated Sub-Working Group to advance the BRICS Integrated Early Warning System for the prevention of and response to mass infectious diseases, together with its Terms of Reference; (ii) encouraging the extension of the scope of the existing Memorandum of Understanding on cooperation in the field of medical products regulation to the regulatory authorities of all BRICS member countries; (iii) welcoming the collective efforts of BRICS regulatory authorities in developing the BRICS GxP White Paper, consolidating best practices of regulatory experience and technical approaches across manufacturing, laboratory and clinical processes; (iv) emphasising stronger health infrastructure and local production of medical products to enhance preparedness for future public health emergencies and ensure timely, affordable and equitable access to vaccines, therapeutics and diagnostics; and (v) supporting the activities of the BRICS Vaccine R&D Centre and recognising the importance of collaboration in nuclear medicine. For India's export-oriented pharmaceutical industry, the twin tracks of regulatory mutual recognition and localised production will simultaneously shape registration pathways and competitive dynamics across multiple markets. — Source: [The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/brics-leaders-call-for-stronger-cooperation-in-disease-surveillance-medical-product-regulation/article71461003.ece)

## 2. Pharma Industry News

**【2026-09-11】Senores Pharmaceuticals' subsidiary Apnar Pharma completes USFDA inspection at Vadodara plant with no GMP-related observations, one minor procedural observation** — Senores Pharmaceuticals informed the stock exchanges that the US FDA conducted a five-day on-site inspection of the formulations manufacturing facility of its subsidiary Apnar Pharma Private Limited in Vadodara, Gujarat, from September 7 to 11, 2026. The outcome reduces near-term regulatory uncertainty for the site — the absence of GMP-related observations means no major compliance risk to production, exports or customer approvals is triggered, though final closure of the procedural observation still depends on the company's response and further communication from the FDA. — Source: [Moneycontrol](https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/business/markets/stock-market-live-updates-nifty50-share-price-sensex-share-price-crude-fii-gift-nifty-rupee-latest-updates-11-09-2026-liveblog-alpha-liveblog-14027505.html)

**【2026-09-12】Imported peritoneal dialysis solution declared "Not of Standard Quality"; Himachal laboratory also flags nine regulatory violations** — A Himachal Pradesh government drug testing laboratory in Baddi declared two batches of an imported peritoneal dialysis solution "Not of Standard Quality (NSQ)": samples drawn from the paediatric intensive care unit and nephrology department of AIIMS-Bilaspur (Himachal Pradesh) did not conform to the bacterial endotoxin standards prescribed in Indian Pharmacopoeia 2022. The product is manufactured in Thailand and imported by Baxter India (whose renal division was spun off in 2024 as the standalone company Vantive). The state health and family welfare department issued the laboratory report on September 3 and wrote to the Drugs Controller General of India (DCGI) on September 7 flagging the findings. The report set out nine violations, the core ones being: key ingredients — sodium chloride, calcium chloride and magnesium chloride — conform to the US Pharmacopeia rather than Indian standards (on which basis the product was classified "misbranded"); sodium lactate appears on the label without any pharmacopoeial specification; the brand name Vantive is displayed in bold and in a larger font size than the generic name and positioned above it, violating the rule that the generic name must be at least two font sizes larger and appear before the brand name; and the label omits the "Single Dose Container" statement, the lower storage-temperature limit and a "Do Not Freeze" warning, the caution that solutions containing visible particles must not be used, and the importer's name, address and import licence number. For companies bringing formulations and APIs into India as imports, this determination again underscores the enforcement intensity applied to three review lines: Indian Pharmacopoeia conformity, labelling compliance, and import-licence traceability. — Source: [ET Pharma](https://pharma.economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/regulatory-update/imported-dialysis-solution-fails-endotoxin-test-in-himachal-lab-flags-nine-regulatory-violations/134101240)

## 4. Risk Alerts

- **USFDA inspections in India remain high-frequency, with "minor procedural observation + zero GMP observation" the prevailing recent outcome**: Senores' subsidiary Apnar Pharma (Vadodara formulations plant, one minor procedural observation) and Aurobindo's subsidiary Apitoria Unit-IV (Pydibhimavaram API plant, zero observations) both completed inspections in the September 7–11 window, adding to last week's disclosures — Alembic's Vadodara BE facility with zero observations, Strides' Chennai formulations plant with three Form 483 observations, Dr. Reddy's Mexico API facility with two observations from a records assessment, and Jubilant Pharmova's US sterile CMO facility classified VAI. Indian customers' requirements on on-site audits, data integrity and change notification for upstream API, intermediate and packaging suppliers will remain elevated through this inspection season; Chinese suppliers serving Indian formulation and sterile customers should keep audit and quality documentation continuously ready.
- **Imported drug compliance scrutiny is tightening, with "Indian Pharmacopoeia conformity + labelling + import-licence traceability" tightening simultaneously**: the Thailand-manufactured imported peritoneal dialysis solution was declared NSQ for failing endotoxin tests and was simultaneously cited for nine violations including use of US Pharmacopeia standards, labelling font-size and warning deficiencies, and a missing import licence number. For imported formulations and APIs that rely on Indian statutory pharmacopoeias for release, ingredient-specific pharmacopoeial applicability, label format (generic name before and two font sizes larger than the brand name), storage and handling warnings, and import-licence traceability should be verified in advance to avoid "misbranded" determinations that can trigger detention or recall.
- **The rupee remains above 95.5 and international crude stays above US$100/bbl, leaving Indian customers' cost and foreign-exchange pressure unrelieved**: on the morning of September 13, USD/INR stood at about 95.55 and Brent crude remained above US$100/bbl (having touched about US$102/bbl last Friday). For an Indian pharmaceutical industry highly dependent on imported APIs, intermediates and KSMs, a weak rupee directly raises the local-currency cost of imported raw materials, while high oil prices simultaneously push up solvent, packaging and logistics costs — buyer pressure on pricing and demands for extended payment terms will stay pronounced in near-term negotiations.

## 5. Tomorrow's Watchlist

- **US Section 232 100% tariff on patented pharmaceuticals/APIs takes effect September 29 for "all other importers"** (generics and biosimilars currently exempt; 15% for the EU/Japan/Korea/Switzerland, 10% for the UK): watch for further Indian drugmakers announcing US onshoring investments or MFN pricing agreements in exchange for relief, and for signals on the start of the one-year review of generic drug tariffs.
- **Pace of implementation of BRICS pharma regulatory cooperation**: the timing of extending the medical products regulatory authorities' MoU to all member countries and of publishing the BRICS GxP White Paper will shape Indian drugmakers' registration mutual recognition and inspection reliance across multiple markets.
- **Three pending domestic regulatory tracks in India**: the Supreme Court hearing on generics-related matters on September 22; the two-month reporting deadline for the three-member committee on a statutory framework for UCPMP; and the CDSCO proposal to bring all sterile drug products under central licensing (CLAA) via amendments to the Drugs Rules, 1945 — all of which will shape compliance pathways and supplier qualification requirements for formulation and sterile customers in India.
