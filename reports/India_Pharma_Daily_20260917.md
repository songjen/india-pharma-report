## 1. Government Pharma Policy

**【2026-09-16】Kerala unveils medical device industry roadmap: FY2031 output target above ₹25,000 crore, plans a "Medical Devices City"** — Chief Minister V.D. Satheesan, inaugurating the Kerala Medical Device Industry Association (KMDIA) in Thiruvananthapuram, said the State will extend full support to new medical device parks and enterprises, and released the KMDIA Strategic Roadmap 2026–2031: the State aims to scale its medical device manufacturing output from the present **₹6,500–7,500 crore (about US$680–780 million)** to **more than ₹25,000 crore (about US$2.61 billion)** by 2031, lifting Kerala's share of India's medical device production from **15–18% to more than 25%**, with expansion into high-value segments including active medical electronics, in-vitro diagnostics (IVD) and orthopaedic implants. The roadmap proposes a **"Medical Devices City" under a public-private partnership**, with plug-and-play manufacturing facilities, ISO Class 5–8 cleanrooms, accredited quality-control laboratories and shared sterilisation facilities to cut the heavy initial investment burden on MSMEs and start-ups; it also proposes closer industry links with SCTIMST, IIT Palakkad, RGCB and NIT Calicut, targeting technology transfer and commercialisation of more than 20 clinical patents. On ease of doing business, the State is moving to a "single door, single document, single approval" system for new industrial projects, with amendments being made to 19 laws, and an Investment Promotion Council chaired by the Chief Minister will clear bottlenecks. — Source: [The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/kerala/keralam-to-support-new-medical-device-parks-fast-track-approvals-says-cm-vd-satheesan/article71473478.ece)

**【2026-09-16】(Update) Maharashtra FDA Commissioner clarifies: not proposing a uniform markup cap, but product-by-product MRP fixation** — Amid the controversy over a proposed 20% cap on medical device margins, Maharashtra Food and Drug Administration Commissioner Tukaram Mundhe said he is "not proposing any cap per se"; MRP would have to be decided **on a product-to-product basis**, but the margin while fixing an MRP had to be "rational, open and transparent", and a gap as large as that between a ₹11 procurement price and a ₹325 MRP could not stand. He declined to name a figure, citing 10%, 20% or 25% only as examples, and stressed that deciding the margin is the role of the NPPA and the industry, while his role as a regulator is to raise the issue. His department has suggested several measures, including **bringing certain products under the Drugs (Prices Control) Order, 2013 (DPCO)**, deciding trade margins, improving inter-agency coordination and strengthening the pricing monitoring mechanism. He maintained any intervention would be a policy-level decision taken in consultation with the pharmaceutical industry, hospitals, regulators and consumers, and would not be unilateral or arbitrary. — Source: [India Today](https://www.indiatoday.in/amp/india/story/hospital-consumables-mrp-gap-maharashtra-fda-rs-11-iv-set-rs-325-2996220-2026-09-16)

**【2026-09-16】Kerala High Court reserves verdict in suo motu case on exorbitant pricing of patented life-saving drugs** — The Kerala High Court (Justice Harisankar V. Menon) on 16 September **reserved its verdict** in the suo motu petition relating to exorbitant pricing of patented life-saving drugs. The case originated from a petition by a breast cancer patient treated with patented **Ribociclib (Novartis)**, priced at about **₹75,000 (about US$782) per month**, seeking intervention by the Central government under the Patents Act to make the drug more affordable; after the petitioner died during the pendency, the Court converted the case into a suo motu petition. The Court examined whether **Section 100 of the Patents Act ("government purposes")** can be invoked without first resorting to the compulsory licensing route under Sections 84 and 92, and whether Palbociclib — no longer under patent and locally manufactured in India — can substitute for patented Ribociclib and Abemaciclib (Eli Lilly). The amicus curiae submitted that Palbociclib is **not a substitute** because of differing toxicity profiles: Ribociclib is prescribed in early-stage disease while Palbociclib is used in metastatic stages, so unaffordability of the patented medicine blocks the right to health of early-stage patients; she also noted that two local companies have obtained CDSCO manufacturing approval but have not applied for compulsory licensing. Novartis and Eli Lilly argued that Section 100 cannot be invoked without first adverting to Sections 84 and 92, and that there is no national emergency where the three substitutable medicines are unavailable; Novartis said Ribociclib is already supplied under a capped price with a 30% trade margin ceiling. — Source: [Live Law](https://www.livelaw.in/high-court/kerala-high-court/kerala-high-court-exorbitant-price-live-saving-patented-drugs-550326)

**【2026-09-15】DGTR publishes list of registered interested parties in anti-dumping probe on dialyzers from China and Malaysia** — India's Directorate General of Trade Remedies (DGTR) published the list of registered interested parties in the anti-dumping investigation concerning imports of **"Dialyzers" originating in or exported from China PR and Malaysia**, ahead of oral hearings and disclosure procedures. Dialyzers are a core consumable in blood purification; the case extends India's recent run of trade remedies against Chinese medical device and consumable imports (after medical examination rubber gloves from Malaysia and Thailand, and moulded soda-lime glass vials from China). — Source: [DGTR](https://www.dgtr.gov.in/en/anti-dumping-investigation-in-india)

## 2. Pharma Industry News

**【2026-09-16】Supreme Court receives PIL seeking three-month investigations, one-year trials for fake drug cases, with mandatory videography** — Lawyer Ashwini Upadhyay filed a public interest litigation (PIL) in the Supreme Court on Tuesday, 15 September, seeking a comprehensive national framework to curb the manufacture and sale of counterfeit and adulterated medicines, including vaccines and cough syrups. Respondents include the Union Ministries of Health and Family Welfare, Law and Justice, and Women and Child Development, all States and Union Territories, and the Law Commission. Key prayers: (i) directions to the Centre and States to complete **investigations into fake medicine cases within three months and trials within one year**; (ii) standard operating procedures for search, seizure and sampling, with **time-bound forensic examination** of seized medicines; (iii) **mandatory digital recording and videography** of search, seizure, sampling and inventory proceedings; (iv) a **graded sentencing policy**; and (v) a Law Commission report proposing changes to existing laws. The plea flagged enforcement gaps under the Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940, saying the absence of uniform investigation procedures, dedicated mechanisms for speedy trials and effective financial investigations has hampered action against organised counterfeit networks, and cited reported incidents including alleged fake cancer drugs supplied to hospitals in Bengaluru and cough syrup-related deaths. — Source: [ET Pharma](https://pharma.economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/policy-and-regulations/pil-in-sc-seeks-time-bound-probe-stringent-action-against-fake-drug-networks/134286694)

**【2026-09-16】Ajanta Pharma's US arm recalls 403,200 fluphenazine tablets after impurity test failure, classified Class III** — According to the latest US FDA enforcement report, Ajanta Pharma's US arm is undertaking a voluntary recall of **403,200 tablets of the antipsychotic fluphenazine hydrochloride** after out-of-specification results for an individual organic impurity: the impurity level (Impurity-A) was recorded at **1.1%, exceeding the specified limit of 1%** during 18-month long-term stability testing. The recall was initiated by the company on **25 August 2026** and is classified as a **Class III** recall (minor defects, exposure not likely to cause adverse health consequences). Fluphenazine hydrochloride tablets are prescribed for severe mental health conditions such as schizophrenia and other psychotic disorders. — Source: [ET Pharma](https://pharma.economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/pharma-industry/ajanta-pharma-recalls-antipsychotic-drug-in-us-after-impurity-test-failure/134281841)

## 3. Key Client Updates

**【2026-09-16】ALKEM LABORATORIES launches NeuCeno (cenobamate) in India — the first cenobamate product in the country** — Alkem Laboratories announced the launch of **NeuCeno (cenobamate)** in India for the treatment of **partial-onset (focal) seizures in adult patients**, marking the first launch of cenobamate in India. NeuCeno is a **once-daily oral therapy** available in strengths from **12.5 mg to 200 mg**; in clinical studies of adults with uncontrolled focal seizures on existing medications, adjunctive cenobamate showed **up to a 78% reduction in seizure frequency**, with **up to 30% of patients achieving seizure freedom** during the maintenance phase. An estimated **9.4 million (94 lakh) people** live with epilepsy in India. — Source: [The Hindu BusinessLine](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/companies/alkem-laboratories-launches-neuceno-for-partial-onset-seizures-in-adults/article71473899.ece) · [Indian Pharma Post](https://www.indianpharmapost.com/news/alkem-launches-neuceno-to-treat-partial-onset-seizures-in-adult-patients-21877)

**【2026-09-15】JUBILANT THERAPEUTICS (Jubilant group): first clinical data for JBI-802 in myeloproliferative neoplasms** — Jubilant Therapeutics Inc., part of the Jubilant group, presented the first clinical data from its ongoing Phase 1/2 study of **JBI-802, a first-in-class oral dual LSD1/HDAC6 inhibitor**, at the Society of Hematologic Oncology (SOHO) 2026 Annual Meeting in Houston. In 12 patients with myeloproliferative neoplasms (MPNs — including essential thrombocythemia, polycythemia vera, myelofibrosis and MDS/MPN), the study reported **rapid, sustained and durable, dose-dependent reductions in platelet counts** with a manageable safety profile. The company said JBI-802 is the only oral dual LSD1/HDAC6 inhibitor currently in clinical development and is administered once daily; enrolment is ongoing, with additional efficacy, safety and molecular response data to follow. — Source: [M2 Pharma](http://m2pharma.com/news/therapy-areas/oncology/article.php?id=108994)

## 4. Risk Alerts

- **Rupee at 95.91 and Brent near US$108/bbl keep the pressure on pricing and payment terms** — USD/INR closed around 95.91 on 16 September (the rupee has depreciated about 6.8% against the dollar this year) and Brent remains above US$108/bbl, with India's crude basket above US$128/bbl, pushing up solvent, packaging and logistics costs. With Indian pharma heavily dependent on imported APIs, intermediates and KSMs, rising local-currency input costs keep buyers' demands for price cuts and longer credit terms strong; with the US Fed rate decision imminent, currency volatility may amplify short-order enquiries and hedging requirements.
- **Tighter enforcement and judicial scrutiny of fake drugs will raise traceability requirements** — The Supreme Court PIL seeks three-month investigations, one-year trials, mandatory videography of search and seizure, and a graded sentencing policy, while the Kerala High Court is scrutinising patented drug pricing. Supply-chain compliance expectations are rising: API and intermediate suppliers to Indian customers should expect stricter batch traceability, packaging and authorised-channel documentation (including QR/barcode) requirements, and should guard against customers pushing inventory from non-compliant channels upstream.
- **Growing uncertainty over the scope of price regulation** — After the Parliamentary Standing Committee on Chemicals and Fertilizers recommended a 20% margin cap on medical devices, the Standing Committee on Health and Family Welfare's 176th Report recommended that high-volume diagnostic and therapeutic devices (advanced pacemakers, ophthalmic lenses, implantable pumps) be systematically evaluated for inclusion in the DPCO scheduled list; the Kerala High Court is also hearing the patented-drug pricing matter. If such recommendations are implemented, procurement price structures in both public and private channels would be reset, and suppliers of raw materials, intermediates and consumables to India should assess channel pricing and annual price-increase headroom in advance.
- **Trade remedies are tightening from both directions** — DGTR's anti-dumping investigation into dialyzers from China and Malaysia has moved to the interested-party registration stage, continuing the expansion of trade remedies on medical devices; combined with the US final anti-dumping determination on glycine from India (57.17%) and India-China trade talks putting the pharma intermediates imbalance back on the agenda, Chinese API/intermediate and medical consumable exporters to India face rising investigation, transhipment-compliance and cost pressure.
