## 1. Government Pharma Policy

**[2026-09-15] Centre proposes easier drug approval norms: BA/BE studies to move to prior-intimation, Form CT-17 import licence timeline halved to 45 working days** — The Indian government is moving to simplify the drug approval process, with two core measures now under deliberation: (1) **bioavailability/bioequivalence (BA/BE) studies to shift to a "prior-intimation" regime** — applicants may start eligible BA/BE studies for domestic registration simply by intimating the drug regulator (CDSCO), instead of requiring prior permission; a similar mechanism was earlier introduced for specified BA/BE studies conducted for export purposes. The proposal has been placed before the **Drugs Technical Advisory Board (DTAB)**, which meets this month. (2) The **Form CT-17 formal import licence** (for import of new drugs and reference products for analytical and non-clinical testing) will see its processing timeline **cut from 90 working days to 45 working days**. These changes follow the Health Ministry's direction to CDSCO to examine Rules 67, 68, 69 and 70 of the New Drugs and Clinical Trials Rules, 2019 in line with an earlier gazette notification, to ease doing business and rationalise the workforce. **High-risk categories are excluded from prior-intimation**: sex hormones, cytotoxic drugs, **beta-lactam antibiotics**, vaccines, cell and gene therapy products, biologics with live microorganisms, and narcotics and psychotropic drugs will retain the existing formal licence requirement. — Source: [Economic Times](https://m.economictimes.com/industry/healthcare/biotech/pharmaceuticals/centre-proposes-easier-norms-for-faster-drug-rollout/amp_articleshow/134247112.cms)

**[2026-09-14] India–China trade talks resume: Goyal meets Commerce Minister Wang Wentao in New Delhi; pharma market access and supply chains top the agenda** — Chinese Commerce Minister Wang Wentao met Indian Commerce and Industry Minister Piyush Goyal in New Delhi over the weekend, after Prime Minister Modi and President Xi concluded their bilateral engagement on the sidelines of the BRICS Summit. China said it stands ready to work with India to implement the consensus reached by the two leaders, expand mutually beneficial economic and trade cooperation and "properly address each other's economic and trade concerns in a balanced manner". The talks focused on the structural trade imbalance, supply-chain issues, and facilitating "meaningful and predictable" market access. India's trade deficit with China widened to **USD 112.1 billion** in FY2025-26 (USD 99.1 billion in the previous fiscal, USD 73.3 billion in FY2021-22), accounting for 33.5% of India's total merchandise trade deficit, driven largely by India's heavy dependence on China for industrial inputs, machinery, electronic components, **chemicals, pharmaceutical intermediates** and critical minerals. India has repeatedly sought greater access for **pharmaceuticals** and agricultural products in China, and Indian businesses have complained of regulatory, sanitary and other non-tariff barriers; China's tighter controls on rare-earth and permanent-magnet exports in 2025 further exposed India's supply-chain vulnerabilities. — Source: [Financial Express](https://www.financialexpress.com/policy/economy-india-china-move-to-address-trade-concerns-as-goyal-meets-wang-4339273/)

**[2026-09-14] BRICS New Delhi Declaration welcomes proposed expert working group on traditional medicine; medicinal-plant and herbal research cooperation elevated** — The Ministry of Ayush said the New Delhi Declaration adopted at the 18th BRICS Summit gives a fresh push to international cooperation in Traditional, Complementary and Integrative Medicine (TCIM). The key outcome is the **welcome accorded to the proposed establishment of a BRICS Expert Working Group on TCIM under the BRICS Health Track**, envisaged as a platform for dialogue, collaboration and exchange among member states and a structured mechanism for advancing cooperation in traditional medicine. The declaration specifically calls for greater cooperation in **research on medicinal plants and herbal preparations** used for therapeutic and preventive purposes, and recognises that the shared traditional pharmacopoeias of BRICS member countries can serve as a foundation for developing safe, effective and affordable therapeutic products. Minister of State (Independent Charge) for Ayush Prataprao Jadhav and Ayush Secretary Vaidya Rajesh Kotecha both stressed advancing Ayurveda and other traditional systems through scientific research, quality assurance and international collaboration. — Source: [ET Pharma](https://pharma.economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/policy-and-regulations/brics-declaration-welcomes-proposed-expert-group-on-traditional-medicine/134230418)

## 2. Pharma Industry News

**[2026-09-14] ICMR: indigenous dengue vaccine DengiAll nears one year of Phase 3 follow-up, unblinding hoped by end-2027** — ICMR Director General Rajiv Bahl, speaking on the sidelines of the 4th Dilip Mahalanabis Oration in Kolkata, said the Phase 3 trial of DengiAll — India's first indigenous, single-dose dengue vaccine — is running at **20 institutes across the country** and has enrolled a little over 13,000 volunteers (randomised 2:1 between vaccine and placebo). Almost a year of follow-up has been completed and the team is **hopeful of unblinding the code by the end of next year**; if all goes well, India could have its own dengue vaccine by the end of next year. DengiAll is expected to work against all four dengue strains prevalent in India. Bahl noted that because dengue cases in India have been generally low this year, the clinical trial team may have to wait longer to unblind the study. — Source: [ET Pharma](https://pharma.economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/pharma-industry/india-may-get-own-dengue-vax-by-next-year-end-icmr-dg/134241696)

## 4. Risk Alerts

- **The simplified approval proposal excludes beta-lactam antibiotics, leaving the registration and import cadence unchanged for our related chain in India**: the BA/BE prior-intimation regime and the halved CT-17 timeline explicitly do not apply to sex hormones, cytotoxic drugs, **beta-lactam antibiotics (penicillins/cephalosporins/carbapenems)**, vaccines, cell and gene therapy, biologics with live microorganisms, and narcotics and psychotropic drugs. Indian customers along the penem-intermediate and cephalosporin chain will continue to face formal licensing and the 90-working-day timeline for BA/BE and CT-17 — combined with the 29 September US Section 232 tariff milestone, registration and release scheduling in India may remain a constraint on procurement cadence.
- **India–China trade talks put pharmaceutical intermediates and supply chains back on the agenda, raising two-way policy risk**: chemicals and pharmaceutical intermediates are a major component of India's USD 112.1 billion FY26 deficit with China; India is seeking greater market access in China for pharmaceuticals even as policy attention to API/KSM import dependence keeps rising. Material progress on tariffs or non-tariff barriers would directly change the procurement costs and competitive landscape for our Indian customers; conversely, if India leans on trade remedies (anti-dumping/anti-subsidy), compliance costs for re-routing Chinese API/intermediates would also rise.
- **Rupee holds near 95.6, keeping imported-input cost and FX pressure on Indian customers**: USD/INR was around 95.61 in the early morning of 15 September. For an Indian pharma industry highly dependent on imported APIs, intermediates and KSMs, a weak rupee directly raises the local-currency cost of imported inputs, and buyer pressure on pricing and demands for extended payment terms remain strong in short-term negotiations.
