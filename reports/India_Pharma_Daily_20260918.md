## 1. Government Pharma Policy

**【2026-09-16】Health Ministry proposes ban on two antibiotic groups for animal use — carboxypenicillins and phosphonic acid derivatives; cephalosporin + beta-lactamase inhibitor combinations still not included** — The Union Ministry of Health and Family Welfare has issued a draft gazette notification (under Section 26A of the Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940, after consultation with the Drugs Technical Advisory Board, DTAB) proposing to prohibit the **import, manufacture, sale, distribution and use of carboxypenicillins and phosphonic acid derivatives, and their formulations, for animal use**. The government is satisfied that veterinary use of such formulations "is likely to involve risk to humans" and that safer alternatives are available, making the prohibition necessary and expedient in the public interest. Background: on 24 April 2025 the DTAB considered a Ministry of Commerce proposal to prohibit 37 antimicrobials for veterinary use, noted that none had CDSCO approval for veterinary use, and recommended banning 34 of them in line with the EU list of antimicrobials reserved for human medicine (18 antibiotics, 18 antivirals, one antiprotozoal). Views of the Department of Animal Husbandry and Dairying (DAHD) were sought on the remaining three groups — **carboxypenicillins, cephalosporin–beta-lactamase inhibitor combinations, and phosphonic acid derivatives**. The current draft covers **only two of the three; cephalosporin + beta-lactamase inhibitor combinations remain outside it**. A 30-day window for objections and suggestions has opened. The move also aligns with EU requirements for animal-origin food imports (Article 118 of Regulation (EU) 2019/6 with Delegated Regulation (EU) 2023/905, effective 3 September 2026), under which exporting countries must provide official guarantees on antimicrobial use. — Source: [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/india-news/centre-plans-ban-on-import-sale-of-two-antibiotics-for-animal-use-in-india-126091601062_1.html)

**【2026-09-16】Health Ministry issues advisory on stem cell therapy: only approved indications as standard care, autism restricted to clinical trials** — The Ministry of Health and Family Welfare has issued an advisory to States and Union Territories reiterating that stem cell research and therapy must follow the existing regulatory framework: stem cell therapy **may be offered as routine standard clinical care only for disease conditions and indications included in the Ministry-approved list**; for **Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD)**, therapeutic use of any type of stem cell **remains restricted to duly approved clinical trials** conducted in accordance with the National Guidelines for Stem Cell Research, 2017, issued jointly by ICMR and the Department of Biotechnology, and must not be offered as routine, standard or commercial clinical services. The advisory follows the Supreme Court judgment of 30 January 2026 (Yash Charitable Trust & Ors. v. Union of India & Ors.) and spells out consequences of non-compliance: professional misconduct under Regulation 7.22 of the IMC Regulations, 2002, and action under Sections 32 and 40 of the Clinical Establishments (Registration and Regulation) Act, 2010, which provide for cancellation of registration and penalties. The National Medical Commission (NMC) issued a similar advisory on 5 September and has asked State Medical Councils to examine alleged violations. — Source: [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/industry/news/govt-tightens-stem-cell-therapy-rules-bars-routine-use-for-autism-126091700663_1.html) · [CNBC TV18](https://www.cnbctv18.com/lifestyle/healthcare/stem-cell-therapy-health-ministry-issues-advisory-restricts-use-for-autism-to-clinical-trials-19992636.htm)

## 2. Pharma Industry News

**【2026-09-16】(Update) Karnataka spurious drug racket: SIT gets custody of key accused, Health Minister seeks murder charges and possible CBI probe** — The Bengaluru spurious drug case has advanced: pharmacy owner Veeresh Kumar Jain (Krupa Healthcare, near Minerva Circle, Bengaluru) was on 16 September **remanded to the custody of the Special Investigation Team (SIT)** to establish his alleged role and the network's operations; prime accused R. Prashanth has left the country (reportedly in Azerbaijan). Case timeline: on 18 August the State Health & Family Welfare Department and the Karnataka Food Safety and Drug Administration raided a farmhouse near the Bidadi toll gate and seized suspected fake and expired medicines worth about **₹4.91 crore (≈US$5.12 million)**, the largest single batch being **5,640 vials labelled Cefiavigent 2.5 injections, valued at ₹3.38 crore**. The modus operandi: **cheap medicines procured from manufacturing clusters in Telangana and Himachal Pradesh were refilled into new vials at Bidadi and relabelled as premium brands of multinational companies** (bought at roughly ₹600–700 a vial, sold for several thousand rupees after relabelling). A six-member SIT was constituted on 19 August under a senior IPS officer, with FSDA and Commercial Taxes officials; the probe now covers **more than 90 hospitals and clinics and over 15 medical representatives**, extending to Rajasthan, Gujarat and Mumbai. State Health Minister U.T. Khader said existing penalties are inadequate and will push for federal amendments bringing counterfeit drug manufacture and sale under **stringent criminal provisions including murder or attempt-to-murder**; he has sought the deputation of central drug surveillance officers or a **CBI probe**. The list of suspected spurious and counterfeit batches has been shared with the Union government and all States, and hospitals and nursing homes in Karnataka have been directed not to administer the seized medicines. — Source: [The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/karnataka/pharmacy-owner-sent-to-sit-custody-in-spurious-drug-racket-case/article71473791.ece) · [The News Minute](https://www.thenewsminute.com/amp/story/karnataka/fake-medicines-should-attract-murder-charges-says-karnataka-health-minister-khader)

**【2026-09-17】Amneal expands India footprint after Kashiv acquisition, adding peptide and biosimilar capacity** — US-based Amneal Pharmaceuticals is expanding its India operations following its August 2026 acquisition of Kashiv BioSciences. The company says it has invested **more than US$300 million in India over 15 years** and now has **over 5,500 employees across eight FDA-approved manufacturing and R&D facilities** (Ahmedabad, Dahej, Visakhapatnam, Hyderabad), covering oral solids, injectables, **APIs**, oncology products and R&D; Kashiv's India subsidiaries add global R&D, clinical, manufacturing, regulatory and IP capabilities. Amneal targets support for **more than 12 commercial biosimilars and over 20 additional pipeline products by 2030**, with multiple biosimilar launches a year, and is **building peptide capacity in Gujarat** (a drug-substance facility in Dahej and drug-product plus drug-substance facilities in Bavla). The company currently markets around 300 generic, biosimilar and specialty medicines with over 160 million prescriptions a year, primarily in the US. — Source: [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/latest/corporate/story/amneal-expands-india-operations-after-kashiv-buy-builds-biosimilars-portfolio-556089-2026-09-17)

## 4. Risk Alerts

- **Veterinary antimicrobial ban expands, but cephalosporin + beta-lactamase inhibitor combinations remain pending**: the current draft covers only carboxypenicillins and phosphonic acid derivatives; the third group (cephalosporin and beta-lactamase inhibitor combinations) still awaits DAHD views. If included, it would directly affect registration and sale of cephalosporin-chain products in India's veterinary channel. Meanwhile, since 3 September 2026 the EU requires official guarantees and attestation on antimicrobial use for animal-origin food imports, tightening documentation requirements across export chains.
- **Spurious drug case exposes "cheap API origin" risk; genuine suppliers must strengthen traceability**: the counterfeit medicines were made from low-cost drugs procured from Telangana and Himachal Pradesh clusters, refilled and relabelled as multinational brands. Combined with the pending Supreme Court PIL seeking mandatory forensic testing and full video recording of raids, requirements for **batch traceability, proof of authorised channels and anti-counterfeiting (including QR codes)** will keep rising. API and intermediate suppliers into India should reinforce CoA, batch and logistics-chain documentation and guard against non-compliant channel inventory being pushed upstream.
- **Rupee near 95.78 with Brent around US$108 and a hawkish Fed keep price, credit-term and hedging pressure in place**: USD/INR was around 95.78 in the morning of 18 September (RBI reference rate 95.89 for 18 September), with the rupee down about 6.8% against the dollar year to date. Brent held above US$108/bbl, pushing up solvent, packaging and logistics costs, while the Fed's hawkish guidance kept the dollar index near a seven-week high. Local-currency import costs for Indian manufacturers remain elevated, so buyer pressure on prices and stretched payment terms persist.
- **Trade remedies against China continue, keeping the chemicals/intermediates export environment tight**: on 15 September the DGTR issued its first sunset review final finding on **sodium hydrosulphite originating in or exported from China**, recommending continuation of the **US$440/tonne** anti-dumping duty for five years (US$300/tonne for Korea in the same case; Indian customs codes 28311010 and 28321020). Although not a pharmaceutical chemical, it combines with the US anti-dumping final ruling on glycine from India (57.17%) to create two-way trade-remedy pressure, raising investigation, transhipment-compliance and cost risks for Chinese raw materials and intermediates bound for India.
- **India's complex-formulation and peptide capacity build-out is front-loading China–India competition**: Amneal is building peptide API and drug-product capacity in Gujarat with more than 12 commercial biosimilars targeted by 2030, adding to Laurus (FY27 capex guidance of ₹2,000 crore) and Neuland (peptide reactor capacity planned to 18,000 L). India's capacity and customer stickiness in complex manufacturing are rising quickly, gradually increasing substitution pressure on Chinese intermediate suppliers at Indian accounts.
