## 1. Government Pharma Policy

### 【2026-08-12】Maharashtra FDA cracks down on Ayurvedic drug makers: licences of 10 firms permanently cancelled, 135 issued show-cause notices (ANI/ET)
The Maharashtra Food and Drug Administration (FDA) conducted a statewide inspection drive covering all 434 licensed Ayurvedic drug manufacturers, assessing compliance with the Drugs and Cosmetics Act 1940, the Drugs and Cosmetics Rules 1945, and Schedule T (GMP for Ayurvedic, Siddha and Unani medicines). Inspections found serious deficiencies including absence of qualified technical personnel, non-functional quality-control departments, unhygienic premises, missing or discrepant batch production and raw-material records, incorrect manufacturing dates on labels, lack of raw-material and finished-product testing, and inadequate cross-contamination controls. **Manufacturing licences of 10 firms were permanently cancelled** for major violations (including Krishna Herbal, Aditya Pharma, Kalidas Gandhi), and **135 manufacturers received show-cause notices**. FDA Commissioner Tukaram Mundhe warned that classical Ayurvedic medicines must be produced per recognised reference texts and patent/proprietary products must meet prescribed quality standards, with strict action against violators. The drive signals tightening state-level GMP enforcement across India.

### 【2026-08-11】Sun Pharma semaglutide tablet launch blocked: Delhi HC requires prior court approval before launch (Novo Nordisk patent suit) (ET Pharma/India Pharma Outlook)
The Delhi High Court has directed Sun Pharma Laboratories that, once it obtains a commercial manufacturing licence for semaglutide tablets (3/7/14 mg, already approved by CDSCO's Subject Expert Committee), it must **first approach the court for approval before any launch**. Novo Nordisk claims its oral semaglutide (Rybelsus, SNAC absorption-enhancer formulation) patent is infringed and sought a restraint order — after its injectable primary patent expired in India in March, generics rushed in, and Novo argued that once launched the drug would be hard to withdraw. Sun countered that the court lacks territorial jurisdiction (HQ in Maharashtra) and that its product is being compared against an expired patent. The case runs parallel to Novo's similar suit against Torrent Pharma, marking a landmark patent barrier for Indian generics entering the oral GLP-1 market (July oral generic sales ~₹11 crore/**$1.15M** vs Novo ₹20 crore/**$2.10M**).
